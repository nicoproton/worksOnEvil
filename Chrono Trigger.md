# Chrono Trigger

***Chrono Trigger*** is a 1995 Japanese role-playing game developed by Square. Its time-travel structure moves across prehistory, antiquity, the Middle Ages, the present, apocalypse, and post-apocalyptic ruin, gradually revealing Lavos as the parasite whose emergence devastates the planet. The strongest connection to this vault is the Zeal/Lavos pattern: a brilliant civilization tries to draw power from a buried world-drainer and is destroyed by what it cannot control.

## Lavos as World-Drainer

Lavos falls to the planet in prehistory and spends millions of years beneath the surface, feeding on planetary energy before emerging in 1999 A.D. to devastate the world. This makes it one of the clearest examples of [[Parasitic World-Drainer]].

Unlike a human conspirator, Lavos does not need ideology. Its evil is ecological and cosmic: it converts the whole history of life into fuel for its own reproduction.

## Zeal Above the Clouds

The Kingdom of Zeal is a floating magical-technological civilization whose elites live above the earth while the Earthbound Ones remain below. Its vertical structure gives it a strong relation to the [[Tower of Babel Archetype]]: height, magical power, class separation, and a desire to draw divine-scale energy into human command.

Zeal's beauty is important. The kingdom is not merely ugly tyranny. It is art, learning, magic, and refinement made dependent on a parasitic source.

Because Zeal's ruin remains legible across later eras, it also belongs to [[Fallen High Civilization]]. Its fall is not caused by ignorance, but by refined power ordered toward a source it cannot master.

## Queen Zeal and the Mammon Machine

Queen Zeal seeks power from Lavos through the Mammon Machine. The project promises transcendence, immortality, and imperial stability, but it is built on contact with a force that feeds on the planet.

Knowledge and ritual technology are aimed at a power the civilization does not understand and cannot master.

The Mammon Machine also belongs to [[Stolen Life-Force Technology]]. Zeal's brilliance depends on drawing power from Lavos, a buried parasite whose energy is inseparable from planetary exploitation.

As inherited danger, the Mammon Machine is also [[Lost Technology as Temptation]]. It concentrates the old kingdom's dream of transcendence into a device that keeps inviting contact with catastrophe.

## Apocalypse and Repair

The ruined future shows the outcome of Lavos's emergence: domes, scarcity, broken machines, and a world after catastrophe. The heroes' mission is not only to defeat a monster but to interrupt a timeline of planetary exhaustion.

That repair-work gives *Chrono Trigger* a useful relation to *Terranigma*. Both games use restoration across history as a playable structure, but *Chrono Trigger* frames the central enemy more explicitly as a buried parasite rather than a dual cosmic principle.

## Time Travel Against Fatalism

The game's time travel makes history revisable without making it simple. The party discovers catastrophe, traces its causes backward, and acts across eras to prevent a future that has already been witnessed.

That structure makes the game a good expansion point for the vault: it adds a temporal version of false salvation and parasitism, where the hidden enemy is not only beneath the earth but distributed across history.
