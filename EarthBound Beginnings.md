# EarthBound Beginnings

***EarthBound Beginnings***, originally released in Japan as *Mother*, is a 1989 role-playing game by Shigesato Itoi and Ape. It follows Ninten, Ana, Lloyd, and Teddy through an America-like world of psychic disturbances, aliens, missing adults, and recovered melodies. Its strongest graph contribution is the idea that wounded evil can be answered not by force, but by remembered love carried in song.

## Giegue and Wounded Memory

Giegue is not introduced as abstract evil alone. He has a history with Ninten's family: George and Maria raised him, and Maria loved him like a child. The final conflict is therefore bound to memory, betrayal, and the pain of a severed maternal bond.

This gives the game a strong relation to [[Privatio Boni]]. Giegue's evil is not creative fullness; it is a wounded and hardened absence of love that cannot bear the memory of having once been loved.

## Magicant as Memory-World

Magicant is eventually revealed as a world created by Maria's consciousness. It is not merely a fantasy realm but a psychic and mnemonic environment, preserving what Maria has forgotten or cannot yet release.

This connects the game to [[Mediated Reality]] and [[Simulated Reality]] in a gentle, interior form. Magicant is not a prison like the Matrix; it is a memory-world that must dissolve once the buried truth is remembered.

## The Eight Melodies

The party gathers the Eight Melodies across the world and finally sings them to Queen Mary, restoring her memory of being Maria. The song then becomes the only way to defeat Giegue.

This makes the game a major anchor for [[Song and Prayer as Binding Force]]. Song is not ornament or mood; it is the carrier of a relation stronger than violence.

## Cannot Be Defeated by Force

In the final battle, ordinary attacks cannot overcome Giegue. The party must sing Maria's lullaby until the memory becomes unbearable to him.

This is close to [[Eucatastrophe]]. Victory comes through the return of a forgotten love, not through technical mastery or superior force.

## Love as the Unanswerable Weapon

The ending does not solve evil by explaining it away. Giegue flees, wounded by memory, and his future revenge remains possible. But the game establishes the series' deepest logic: the weakest-seeming bonds, song and remembered care, can reach what power cannot.
