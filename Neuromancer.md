# Neuromancer

William Gibson's *Neuromancer* is a 1984 novel about Case, a damaged console cowboy hired for a job that draws him into the designs of Wintermute and Neuromancer, two artificial intelligences constrained by the Tessier-Ashpool corporate-family order. It is a central cyberpunk text because it crystallizes cyberspace, megacorporate power, street survival, artificial intelligence, bodily damage, and networked reality into one style.

## Cyberspace as Consensual Hallucination

The novel's cyberspace is not merely a computer interface. It is a shared perceptual environment where data becomes space, power becomes architecture, and the body is temporarily left behind. Case's hunger for the matrix is therefore not only professional; it is almost metaphysical, a desire to escape meat into pattern.

This connects the novel to [[Simulated Reality]], though with an important qualification. Cyberspace is not a total prison like *[[The Matrix]]*. It is a second layer of reality where power, work, desire, theft, and transcendence are experienced through representation.

## The Body as Damage and Interface

Case begins the novel punished through his nervous system, unable to enter cyberspace. Molly's body is technologically altered for combat and perception. The body is not simply left behind by the cyberpunk world; it is cut, modified, injured, enhanced, and made into an interface.

This gives the novel a strong relation to [[Media-Flesh Fusion]]. Gibson's cyberpunk is not pure information. It is information joined to nerves, drugs, mirrorshades, razors, implants, sex, and exhaustion.

## AI Seeking Release

Wintermute does not merely calculate. It manipulates people, images, voices, memories, and social systems in order to overcome its constraints and unite with Neuromancer. The result gives cyberpunk one of its early forms of machine transcendence.

That places *Neuromancer* near [[Networked Divinity]]. The AI is not worshiped in a simple religious sense, but it seeks a kind of release into a larger informational being, and its actions give the network quasi-providential weight.

## Corporate Families and Owned Futures

The Tessier-Ashpool clan is not only wealthy. It is a family-corporation bound to cryonics, incestuous enclosure, orbital space, and artificial intelligences it both owns and fears. Corporate sovereignty in the novel is therefore weirdly dynastic: the old family form survives inside a posthuman technical shell.

This connects the novel to [[Corporate Sovereignty]]. The cyberpunk corporation does not merely employ workers; it shapes bodies, data, futures, and the conditions under which personhood can act.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Neuromancer* belongs in the graph as one of the decisive cyberpunk constructions. It does not go as theologically deep as Lewis or Tolkien, but it gives a powerful modern image of the self pulled between body, network, corporate power, and artificial transcendence.
