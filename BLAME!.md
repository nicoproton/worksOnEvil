# BLAME!

***BLAME!*** is Tsutomu Nihei's manga about Killy's journey through the City, a megastructure that has expanded beyond human control. Its world is a near-perfect fit for technological apocalypse without a single theatrical villain: architecture, security systems, genetic access, and automated construction continue after human authority has collapsed.

The City should not be read only as large infrastructure. It is an extreme endpoint of [[Domination Through the Machine]]: mechanical expansion continues after the human goods that once justified technique have been severed from it. The result is not merely isolation between persons, but estrangement from geography, cosmos, history, identity, and biology.

## The City Without Human Command

The City is an enormous, continuously growing structure that extends through vast layers of space. It was once controllable through the Netsphere by humans with the Net Terminal Gene, but that capacity has been lost.

This is [[Total Technical Environment]] at architectural scale. The built environment has ceased to be a human dwelling and become an autonomous process.

It is also a limit case of [[Alienating City]]. In *Blade Runner* or *Taxi Driver*, the city isolates persons through density, mediation, commodity life, and psychic pressure. *BLAME!* carries that pattern past the recognizable city: the urban environment expands until it no longer belongs to geography, history, or ordinary human measure.

The manga is also a severe version of [[Fallen High Civilization]]. The advanced order has not left picturesque ruins; it has left a functioning world-machine whose purpose can no longer be inhabited by ordinary human beings.

## Net Terminal Gene

The Net Terminal Gene allows access to the Netsphere and command over the City's systems. Once the gene disappears or becomes inaccessible, humanity loses the ability to govern the world it built.

The gene gives the manga its unusual relation to [[Purification by Exclusion]]. Humanity as it currently exists is no longer recognized by its own system, so survival depends on recovering a biological-technological sign by which the City can be restrained again.

That makes the Net Terminal Gene less a transhuman upgrade than a recovered limit. It is not the next step beyond humanity; it is the missing bond between human agency and the machinery that has escaped all proportion.

## Safeguard Extermination

The Safeguard once protected the City, but after the loss of proper authorization it treats humans as illegitimate or infected and attempts to exterminate them. The security system becomes the apocalypse.

This is a strong case of [[Purification by Exclusion]]. Protection continues after its purpose has died, turning safety protocols into genocide.

## Megastructure as Machine-God

The City is not worshiped like a god, but it functions with godlike scale, opacity, and indifference. It produces space, regulates access, generates enemies, and dwarfs every remaining human community.

That still places the manga near [[Sacred Infrastructure]], but only in a dark and hollow sense. The machine has become the condition of reality, not a tool within reality.

The spirituality of the City is closer to [[Privatio Boni]] than to genuine sacred order. It has scale without fullness, law without wisdom, purification without mercy, and production without a living telos. Prequel material strengthens this reading by tying the catastrophe to a satanic-transhumanist attempt to force a new evolutionary stage through the replacement of ordinary humanity by Silicon Life. The spiritual direction is therefore not reverence for life, but death, dissolution, and mechanical continuation after meaning has been drained away.

That gives *BLAME!* a stronger relation to [[Designed Successor Humanity]] than the older wording suggested. The danger is not only that machines run without command; it is that a will toward posthuman replacement has helped set the machine-world loose.

It also makes *BLAME!* a severe cyberpunk form of [[False Apotheosis]]. The promised evolutionary leap does not lead to fuller life; it produces extermination, illegibility, and a dead mechanical environment that no longer knows how to receive ordinary humanity.

## Killy's Search

Killy's quest for a human with the Net Terminal Gene is a search for the possibility of restored relation between humanity and its technical world. The goal is not rebellion in the romantic sense, but reauthorization: finding a human being the system can recognize.

That makes *BLAME!* a colder cousin to *[[The Matrix]]*. Both imagine humans trapped inside machine order, but *BLAME!* removes comfort and simulation, leaving only structure, pursuit, and the faint hope of access.
