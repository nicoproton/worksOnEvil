# The Sorrows of Young Werther

***The Sorrows of Young Werther*** is Johann Wolfgang von Goethe's 1774 epistolary novel about Werther, a sensitive young man whose love for Charlotte becomes absolute even though she is promised to Albert. It belongs in this graph as a sentimental and Romantic form of life lived through inward intensity.

## Feeling as Absolute Measure

Werther's inward life is rich, lyrical, and responsive to beauty, nature, art, and affection. The danger is that feeling becomes the final measure of reality.

This makes the novel a strong case of [[Bovarysme]]. Werther does not simply love Charlotte; he imagines himself through the role of the suffering lover, and that role begins to govern what life can mean.

## Love Without Order

Werther's love attaches itself to a real good. Charlotte is not merely a fantasy object, and Werther's longing is not trivial. The tragedy comes when love is severed from patience, renunciation, friendship, and the limits of another person's life.

This places the novel near [[Disordered Love]]. Feeling becomes destructive not because feeling is false, but because it is loved beyond the order that could make it fruitful.

## The Letter as Self-Enclosure

The epistolary form matters. Werther's letters make his inward life vivid, but they also enclose him in his own interpretation. He narrates himself until his suffering becomes the story he must complete.

This gives the novel a sentimental form of [[Aesthetic Self-Enclosure]]. The self becomes beautiful to itself as suffering, and that beauty becomes difficult to abandon.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Werther* belongs because it deepens the branch between *[[Don Quixote]]* and *[[Madame Bovary]]*. Cervantes shows life captured by romance books; Goethe shows life captured by the aesthetic and moral prestige of feeling.
