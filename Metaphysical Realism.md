# Metaphysical Realism

Metaphysical Realism names the Catholic and Thomistic pattern in which thought begins from being rather than from system, method, will, consciousness, language, or utility. Reality is not first raw material for construction or a projection of the mind. It is given, intelligible, and prior to the systems that try to explain it.

This bridge belongs under [[Reason Ordered to Truth]] and beside [[Thomas Aquinas]]. It is useful when the graph needs a precise positive counter-form to idealism, positivism, skepticism, and system-building.

Use this link when:

- philosophy is called back to being, existence, act, participation, causality, and created reality;
- a system fails because it begins from method, consciousness, essence, history, or science rather than being;
- Catholic thought defends realism without reducing reason to empirical technique;
- metaphysics protects theology, anthropology, and moral judgment from collapse into fashion or ideology.

Etienne Gilson's *[[Being and Some Philosophers]]* is the central Gilsonian anchor. The book follows how philosophers understand being and argues for the Thomistic priority of existence.

*[[The Unity of Philosophical Experience]]* gives the historical-pattern form. Gilson reads philosophical history as a series of experiments where departures from being tend to end in skepticism, positivism, or anti-metaphysical closure.

*[[Aeterni Patris]]* gives the ecclesial restoration form through Thomas Aquinas. The point is not antiquarian nostalgia, but a recovery of first principles strong enough to judge novelty.

Romano Guardini's *[[The World and the Person]]* gives an anthropological neighbor: world and person are not self-created projections, but received realities that thought must answer.

This concept should not be used for any realistic tone or empirical description. It belongs where being as such, or philosophy's relation to being, is doing real work.
