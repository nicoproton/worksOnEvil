# Reincarnation and Cycles

Reincarnation and Cycles names works where death, rebirth, recurrence, or historical repetition structures the story. The motif can be religious, cosmic, psychological, or narrative.

In this vault, it is most useful when recurrence is not just background mythology but the form through which a work thinks about responsibility. Characters may be punished by repetition, healed through acceptance of death, or forced to see that immortality without transformation is a trap.

This concept often contrasts with [[Artificial Immortality]]. Instead of preserving the same self indefinitely, cyclical stories ask what changes, returns, or must be relinquished.

The concept can apply to individual souls, as in *[[Phoenix (Tezuka)]]*, or to civilizations, as in *[[A Canticle for Leibowitz]]*, where history rebuilds toward the same destructive threshold.

In *[[Xenogears]]*, recurrence binds identity, trauma, and resistance across history. In *[[Nausicaa of the Valley of the Wind (Manga)]]*, the cycle is ecological and civilizational, shaped by the long aftermath of old-world catastrophe.
