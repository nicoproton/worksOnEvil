# The Artwork of the Future

***The Artwork of the Future*** is Richard Wagner's 1849 essay developing his vision of a total artwork that reunites music, poetry, drama, dance, staging, myth, and communal participation. It belongs in this graph because it gives an aesthetic version of total formation: the future community is to be shaped through an art that acts almost like a social-spiritual organism.

## The Total Artwork

Wagner's ideal is not a single art among others, but a synthesis. The separated arts are to recover their unity in a dramatic work capable of addressing the whole person and the whole community.

This connects the essay to [[World-Consciousness Formation]] in an aesthetic rather than administrative sense. The work does not form a planetary bureaucracy, but it does imagine shared consciousness being shaped through symbolic, bodily, musical, and mythic participation.

## Communal Body and Myth

The future artwork depends on a people capable of receiving itself through myth. Wagner's art is therefore not merely private expression. It is an attempt to create or restore a communal body.

This places the essay near [[Religion of Humanity]] cautiously. The sacred function of art is redirected toward human community, culture, and collective renewal.

## Total Art and Sub-Creation

The essay is useful beside Tolkien's [[Sub-Creation]] by contrast. Both Wagner and Tolkien take mythic making seriously, but Tolkien's good sub-creation invites free participation in a secondary world, while Wagner's total artwork risks becoming a complete aesthetic environment for communal absorption.

That does not make Wagner simply manipulative. It makes him important for the graph's recurring question: when does making a world become a way of forming the persons who enter it?

*[[Bauhaus Manifesto and Program]]* gives a later architectural and pedagogical version of the total-art impulse. The arts are to be reunited around building, not around music drama, and the school becomes the workshop where that unity is trained.

## Why It Belongs Here

*The Artwork of the Future* belongs because it shows how revolution, myth, body, and art can become one project. It is not technocratic, but it anticipates later anxieties about total environments that shape feeling and identity.
