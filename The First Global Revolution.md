# The First Global Revolution

***The First Global Revolution*** is a 1991 report by Alexander King and Bertrand Schneider for the Club of Rome. It argues that humanity faces a global problematique: interlocking crises that cannot be solved by old national, economic, or ideological frameworks. It is useful to this vault because it frames planetary crisis as requiring new forms of global consciousness and coordination.

## The Problematique

The report's key concept is the problematique: a tangled set of global problems including environment, population, development, security, governance, and cultural change. The problems are linked, so isolated solutions fail.

This makes the book a major anchor for [[Global Problematique]]. It gives non-fiction vocabulary to the systemic scale already visible in ecological and technocratic works like *[[Nausicaa of the Valley of the Wind (Manga)]]* and *[[Between Two Ages]]*.

## The Resolutique

The report also speaks of the resolutique: the search for integrated solutions to the problematique. The conceptual danger is built in. If the crisis is global and interdependent, the proposed response tends toward global coordination, planning, and consciousness-shaping.

This connects the book to [[False Salvation Through Systems]]. The problems may be real, but the system that claims to solve them can become a new source of domination.

## Common Enemy Logic

The report is often discussed for its claim that humanity may need a common motivation or common enemy to unite around global problems. Environmental crisis, pollution, climate, or scarcity can become that unifying pressure.

This gives the book a strong connection to [[Engineered Consent]]. A crisis can be framed not only as a fact to be addressed, but as a symbolic instrument for mobilizing global agreement.

## World Consciousness and Governance

The book's deeper interest is the formation of a planetary consciousness adequate to planetary problems. This puts it beside [[Elite Network Historiography]]: global systems require institutions, experts, and narratives capable of organizing large populations.

For the graph, the report should be handled carefully. It is not a fictional villain plan. It is a real-world systems-governance text whose relevance lies in the ambiguity between necessary coordination and managed consent.
