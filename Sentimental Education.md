# Sentimental Education

***Sentimental Education*** is Gustave Flaubert's 1869 novel about Frederic Moreau, a young man whose romantic, social, artistic, and political ambitions drift through the world of Paris and the Revolution of 1848. It belongs in this graph as Flaubert's male and historical counterpart to *[[Madame Bovary]]*.

## Romance Without Entry

Frederic's love for Madame Arnoux shapes his imagination of life, but his passion rarely becomes decisive action. He lives among possibilities, memories, social chances, and imagined futures.

This makes the novel a subtler case of [[Bovarysme]]. Emma Bovary tries to force life to become romance; Frederic often lets life pass while preserving the image of romance as a private measure.

## Historical Event as Missed Reality

The 1848 Revolution gives the novel a larger stage, but Frederic does not become a tragic hero of history. Politics, friendship, career, money, and desire pass through him without forming a coherent vocation.

This connects the novel to [[Mediated Reality]] in a historical-literary form. Frederic experiences public life through poses, conversations, reputations, and inherited political images more than through real commitment.

## Sentiment as Drift

The title is bitterly exact. Frederic is educated by sentiment, but the education does not make him wise. It trains him in disappointment, nostalgia, and the preservation of unrealized possibility.

This is close to [[Disordered Love]], though less violently than in *Madame Bovary*. The problem is not one spectacular transgression, but a life dissolved by desires never ordered into truthful action.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Sentimental Education* belongs because it shows Bovarysme without Emma's dramatic collapse. The false life can be not only a passionate rebellion against reality, but a long habit of never entering reality fully.
