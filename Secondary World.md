# Secondary World

Secondary World is Tolkien's term for a made imaginative world with inner consistency. A successful secondary world does not merely ask for suspension of disbelief; it produces secondary belief, a temporary but serious participation in the world's logic.

## Inner Consistency

For Tolkien, fantasy requires reason. The secondary world must hold together. Its marvels are believable not because they imitate the primary world, but because they obey the world's own order.

This makes the concept useful for understanding [[Sub-Creation]]. A secondary world is not an arbitrary escape; it is a crafted order.

## Contrast With Simulation

Secondary World should be distinguished from [[Simulated Reality]]. A secondary world invites imaginative participation while leaving the reader free. A simulated reality inside a dystopian work often conceals, imprisons, or manipulates.

This contrast helps separate Tolkienian fantasy from works like *[[The Matrix]]* and *[[Serial Experiments Lain]]*. Both involve alternative realities, but their moral structures differ.

## Applicability, Not Allegory

Tolkien's preference for applicability over imposed allegory belongs here. A secondary world is most powerful when it is not reduced to a one-to-one code. It can illuminate many situations because it remains internally alive.

This principle also protects the vault's method: links should reveal resonances without forcing every work into a single thesis.
