# Mediated Reality

Mediated Reality names the condition in which people act through representations of the world rather than direct access to the world itself. News, images, statistics, stereotypes, maps, narratives, and expert reports form the practical environment in which political judgment happens.

## Pseudo-Environment

Walter Lippmann's *[[Public Opinion]]* gives the concept its strongest anchor through the pseudo-environment. Modern publics cannot directly experience complex society, so they act on mental pictures created through mediation.

This differs from [[Simulated Reality]]. A mediated reality need not be a total artificial world; it may be ordinary public life under conditions of scale, distance, and media dependence.

In literary form, mediation can happen before electronic media. [[Bovarysme]] names the case where novels, social images, fashions, and romantic scripts become the practical environment through which a person judges ordinary life.

## Political Use

Mediated reality becomes dangerous when the available pictures of the world are engineered, narrowed, or staged. The public may still feel informed while responding to an environment shaped by others.

This links the concept to [[Engineered Consent]], [[Language as Control System]], and [[False Salvation Through Systems]].

In [[Technetronic Society]], mediated reality becomes electronic and informational: social perception is shaped through communication systems, images, data, and expert interpretation.

Philip K. Dick intensifies the concept inwardly. In *[[VALIS]]*, the problem is not only media but revelation, paranoia, and the possibility that history itself is a false overlay.

In *[[The Penultimate Truth]]*, mediation becomes spatial and political: underground workers live according to an official picture of the surface war, while the real surface is withheld from them.

*[[Stand on Zanzibar]]* gives the social-overload form: public reality arrives through headlines, reports, slogans, ads, vignettes, documents, and expert language until the novel's form imitates a mediated civilization. *[[The Atrocity Exhibition]]* gives the media-trauma form: images of war, celebrity, medicine, sex, and violence reorganize psychic life. *[[Videodrome]]* gives the screen-body form, where broadcast media enters perception and flesh. *[[Backrooms]]* gives the internet-myth form: an image, creepypasta text, found-footage video, and shared lore make an impossible architectural environment feel discovered rather than authored. *[[American Psycho]]* gives the commodity-surface form, where brands, status images, and media language become the practical environment of personhood. *[[The Shockwave Rider]]* gives the information-age form, where records and networks mediate identity itself. *[[RoboCop]]* gives the commercial-satirical form, where public violence, politics, and corporate action are packaged as news and advertisement.

The Mother series gives the concept a dreamlike game form. Magicant in *[[EarthBound Beginnings]]* is a memory-world, while Moonside in *[[EarthBound]]* is a local collapse of shared meaning.

Japanese media works add several forms of mediated selfhood. *[[Paranoia Agent]]* turns rumor, television, and mascot culture into a contagious social reality, while *[[Perfect Blue]]* shows an idol-image becoming more socially powerful than the performer herself. *[[Pulse (Kairo)]]* turns screen-mediated contact into haunting rather than communion. *[[Welcome to the N.H.K.]]* gives the hikikomori form, where media scripts and conspiracy fantasy filter social life before encounter can happen. *[[Helter Skelter (Okazaki)]]* shows the celebrity body being remade for an image-world that becomes more socially real than the person.

[[Inward Life as Data]] names the sharper case where mediation reaches thoughts, memories, perceptions, or desires directly. *[[Feed]]* gives the consumer-brain form, while *[[The Fortunate Fall]]* gives the historical-media form.
