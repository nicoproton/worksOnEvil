# Corpus Mysticum

***Corpus Mysticum*** is Henri de Lubac's 1944 study of the Eucharist, the Church, and the medieval history of the phrase "mystical body." It belongs in this graph because it gives a sacramental answer to false unity: the Church is not made by ideology, administration, race, class, or technique, but by Christ's Eucharistic gift.

Like *[[Surnaturel]]*, this work was caught up in the mid-century controversy around de Lubac, though it later became highly influential for eucharistic ecclesiology. The node should therefore be read as a theological work with an important reception history, not as a standalone magisterial definition.

## Eucharist and Church Belong Together

De Lubac's famous recovery is that Eucharist and Church must not be separated. The Eucharist makes the Church, and the Church celebrates the Eucharist. Communion is not merely private devotion, nor is the Church merely an external institution that happens to possess a rite.

This makes the book central for [[Ecclesial Communion]]. It gives the sacramental core of the concept.

## Against Merely Institutional Religion

The book helps the graph distinguish Catholic ecclesial order from bureaucracy. The Church has visible form, hierarchy, doctrine, and discipline, but her unity is not produced the way a state or organization produces compliance.

This gives the work a contrast with [[Hollowed Order]]. Ecclesial form becomes hollow when sacramental mystery is reduced to administration, identity, or cultural habit.

## A Counter-Unity to Modern Systems

Many modern nodes in the vault show unity produced by planning, propaganda, shared crisis, technical environment, or collective myth. *Corpus Mysticum* gives a different unity: persons are united by receiving Christ, not by being standardized.

That is why it also belongs near [[Embodied Relation]]. Eucharistic communion is not an idea-only unity. It involves body, bread, wine, altar, worship, and the concrete gathered Church.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Corpus Mysticum* is a strong de Lubac addition because it prevents the Catholic branch from becoming only philosophical critique. It shows the positive center: communion is sacramental before it is sociological.
