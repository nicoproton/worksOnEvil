# Rights of Man

***Rights of Man*** is Thomas Paine's 1791-1792 defense of the French Revolution and reply to Edmund Burke, attacking hereditary government and arguing for natural rights, republican reform, and social measures against poverty. It belongs in this graph as the clearest Paine node for revolutionary progress against inherited political order.

## Against Burkean Inheritance

Paine rejects the idea that the dead can bind the living through inherited political arrangements. Government must answer to present rights and reason, not to tradition treated as sacred possession.

This gives the work a strong relation to [[Progress as Secular Providence]]. The future is authorized to judge the past, and political legitimacy is relocated from inherited form to rational rights.

## Reform, Welfare, and the People

Paine's radicalism is not only negative. He argues for forms of social provision and public support so that political liberty has material conditions.

This connects the work to [[False Salvation Through Systems]] only cautiously. Paine is not building a total managed society, but he does make the state an instrument for relieving suffering and reorganizing inherited inequality.

## Hollow Nobility

Aristocracy and hereditary monarchy appear as forms emptied of public good. They preserve privilege while claiming legitimacy through ancestry.

That places the work near [[Hollowed Order]]. Paine's attack is not simply envy of rank; it is a claim that old forms can survive after justice has departed from them.

## Revolutionary Humanitarianism

The work helps explain why modern revolution can feel moral rather than merely political. Rights, poverty relief, representation, and anti-aristocratic anger combine into a public ethic of emancipation.

This makes *Rights of Man* useful beside *[[Political Justice]]* and *[[A New View of Society]]*. Godwin supplies philosophical perfectibility, Owen supplies environmental formation, and Paine supplies public revolutionary rhetoric.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Rights of Man* belongs because it gives the graph the rights-based, anti-hereditary political strand behind later arguments about progress, reform, and the legitimacy of breaking inherited order.
