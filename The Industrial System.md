# The Industrial System

***The Industrial System*** (*Du systeme industriel*, 1821-1822) is Henri de Saint-Simon's major statement of industrial society as a new social order. It belongs in this graph because it gives an early source for the idea that society should be reorganized around production, science, industry, and expert administration.

Saint-Simon is not a modernist architect, but his importance for the architecture branch is indirect and deep. He helps create the atmosphere in which engineers, industrialists, scientists, planners, and organizers can appear as the rightful leaders of the new age.

## Productive Society

The work shifts attention from inherited aristocratic rank toward productive function. The useful classes are those who work, invent, organize, build, finance, administer, and improve material life.

This places the book under [[Life Redesigned by Ideology]]. Society is no longer imagined chiefly as an inherited order of estates, locality, church, custom, and crown. It becomes a system of productive capacities to be coordinated for improvement.

## Administration Replaces Politics

Saint-Simon's industrialism tends to replace older political conflict with administration by competent organizers. The question becomes how to direct social energy toward production, welfare, and collective improvement.

This is one ancestor of [[Scientific World State]]. Later technocratic and planning movements will make the same move more explicitly: public life should be governed by knowledge, expertise, coordination, and useful work rather than by inherited authority or faction.

## Engineers and Planners

The book helps explain why the engineer becomes such a powerful modern figure. In later architectural modernism, the engineer can appear as someone closer to truth than the traditional architect: less ornamental, less historical, more exact, more useful.

That makes the work a remote but real ancestor of [[Machine for Living]] and [[High-Modernist Planning]]. The point is not direct influence on every architect. The point is the older ideological prestige of production, system, industry, and expert coordination.

## Why It Belongs Here

*The Industrial System* belongs because it gives the graph one of the deep ideological roots of modern planning. Before the Bauhaus, Le Corbusier, or Soviet Constructivism, Saint-Simon helps imagine society itself as an industrial system to be rationally organized by productive elites.
