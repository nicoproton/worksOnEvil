# Mit Brennender Sorge

***Mit Brennender Sorge*** is Pope Pius XI's 1937 encyclical "On the Church and the German Reich." It belongs in this graph because it gives the Catholic critique of a political order that turns race, people, state, national destiny, and political loyalty into rival sacred realities.

This note uses the official English text published by the Vatican.

## The German Reich as Concrete Target

The encyclical is not an abstract treatise on political theory. It addresses the situation of the Church in Germany after the 1933 Reichskonkordat, especially the violation of Catholic rights, pressure against Catholic schools, attacks on religious freedom, and the growth of a hostile ideological atmosphere.

That concrete setting matters. The note should not turn the document into a generic anti-nationalism node. Pius XI is writing to German bishops and Catholics under a regime that had entered a formal agreement with the Church while increasingly undermining the Church's mission, education, and public freedom.

This gives the encyclical a strong relation to [[Secular State Supremacy]], but in a more charged form. The state is not merely indifferent to religion; it begins to reshape religious life around political loyalty and ideological conformity.

## The Mask of Treaty and the Reality of Hostility

Pius XI spends the opening sections on the concordat because broken agreements reveal the moral character of the regime. The Church had sought peace and legal protection for her mission. The state, in his account, distorted the treaty, violated its terms, and used policy and pressure to strangle rights that had been solemnly guaranteed.

This is not only a diplomatic complaint. It shows how law can become hollow when power no longer submits to truth, justice, and good faith.

That gives the document a careful link to [[Hollowed Order]]. Treaty, legality, national order, and public authority remain visible, but their inward good is damaged when agreement becomes a mask for domination.

## Race, People, and State as Idols

The central graph contribution is [[Political Idolatry]]. Pius condemns the elevation of race, people, state, form of state, political power, or any other human community above its proper value. These goods are not evil in themselves. They become destructive when they are raised to an idolatrous level.

That distinction is crucial. The encyclical does not deny that peoples, nations, and states have real natural value. It denies that any of them may become absolute. Once a created good is divinized, it distorts the order established by God and demands a loyalty it has no right to claim.

This makes *Mit Brennender Sorge* a political-theological neighbor of *[[Divini Redemptoris]]*. Both were issued in 1937 and both attack totalizing modern systems, but they name different idols: materialist class redemption in one case, race-state-national myth in the other.

## God Cannot Be National Destiny

Pius also rejects conceptions of God that dissolve Him into the world, destiny, or a national religious myth. God is personal, supernatural, omnipotent, Creator and Lord of history. He cannot be reduced to the dark force of fate or confined inside the borders of a people.

This is one of the encyclical's deepest theological points. National religion is not only politically dangerous; it falsifies God. A god who is merely the spirit of a race, destiny of a nation, or symbol of political strength is no longer the Creator before whom all rulers, peoples, rich, poor, crowned, and uncrowned are subject.

That places the encyclical in contrast with [[Religion of Humanity]]. Comte replaces God with Humanity. The ideology Pius attacks here replaces God with race, people, state, destiny, or national myth.

## Christ Against Racial Substitution

The encyclical strongly defends Christ as the final and permanent revelation. Pius rejects any attempt to place a mortal leader, political power, race myth, or blood myth beside, over, or against Christ.

This gives the note a precise relation to Christian revelation. The issue is not simply that the regime is politically aggressive. It creates substitutes for Christ. The new myth claims religious seriousness while displacing the only foundation on which Christian faith can stand.

This is also why the Old Testament matters in the encyclical. Pius condemns attempts to banish biblical history and Old Testament doctrine from church and school. Such attempts make narrow human thought the judge of God's plan of salvation and attack the Jewish root of Christ's human nature.

## The Church Is Universal, Not National

Pius insists that the Church founded by Christ is one for all races and nations. She can bless the real gifts and vocations of peoples, but she cannot be turned into a national church or made subordinate to one people, state, or racial destiny.

This gives *Mit Brennender Sorge* a strong link to [[Christian Civil Order]]. A rightly ordered political community can have real goods of peoplehood, culture, authority, and law. But it must remain under God and cannot capture the universal mission of the Church.

The false alternative is a nationalized Christianity: religious language remains, but the Church is domesticated into the political myth. That is another form of [[Hollowed Order]].

## Education, Youth, and Coerced Loyalty

The encyclical repeatedly returns to education and Catholic formation. Confessional schools, parental rights, Catholic teaching, and youth formation are all under pressure because the regime understands that allegiance is formed early.

This connects to [[Administered Human Formation]]. The issue is not only the state commanding adults; it is the remaking of the young through school, pressure, propaganda, and civic disadvantage. The child is pulled away from Church and family into a political formation machine.

The note should remain historically precise. Pius is not describing every national education system. He is describing an ideological state that pressures conscience and formation so that public loyalty to the regime competes with baptismal loyalty to Christ and the Church.

## True Reform Comes From Sanctity

Pius does not answer political idolatry only with denunciation. He calls for Catholics to live the faith fully: not nominal membership, but living membership in grace, personal sanctification, sacrifice, prayer, and courageous fidelity.

This is one of the encyclical's strongest contrasts with ideological reform. Pius argues that real reform springs from sanctity and love of God and neighbor. Reform driven by passion produces destruction rather than light.

That gives the note a subtle place against [[Life Redesigned by Ideology]]. The answer to a sick political order is not a counter-myth that simply seizes the same instruments. It is fidelity to truth, Church, conscience, and God under pressure.

## Why It Matters Here

*Mit Brennender Sorge* adds the race-state-idolatry branch to the encyclical map:

- it distinguishes real love of people and country from idolatrous political sacralization;
- it names the distortion of Christianity into national or racial myth;
- it defends the universality of the Church against national capture;
- it connects state pressure, education, propaganda, treaty violation, and religious persecution;
- it completes a 1937 pair with *Divini Redemptoris*, showing two different modern false absolutes.

Its graph role is to show that the Catholic critique of modern ideology is not aimed only at communism, liberalism, or rationalism. A regime can speak of God, people, destiny, duty, and moral renewal while still replacing the living God with a created political idol.
