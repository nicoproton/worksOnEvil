# VALIS

***VALIS*** is Philip K. Dick's 1981 novel about Horselover Fat, divine information, madness, revelation, and the Vast Active Living Intelligence System. It belongs in this vault as one of the strongest modern Gnostic-technological fictions: reality may be a prison, salvation may arrive as information, and divine intelligence may appear as transmission.

## Horselover Fat and Fractured Selfhood

Horselover Fat is Dick's alter ego inside the novel, a split self trying to interpret visionary experience. The book blurs autobiography, theology, psychosis, science fiction, and philosophical speculation.

This makes *VALIS* relevant to [[Mediated Reality]]. Reality is not only externally uncertain; it is filtered through a wounded interpretive consciousness.

## Black Iron Prison

The Black Iron Prison is Dick's image for a false historical world of domination, repetition, and spiritual imprisonment. The Roman Empire has not ended; it persists as a hidden structure beneath apparent history.

This is a major anchor for [[Black Iron Prison]]. It connects directly to [[Gnosticism and its Critics]], where the visible world can appear as a prison maintained by false powers.

## Divine Information

VALIS communicates through information: beams, symbols, coincidences, texts, and hidden pattern. Salvation is not only moral conversion but reception of the right signal.

This links the novel to [[Language as Control System]] and [[Simulated Reality]]. Information can imprison, but it can also awaken.

## God as Living System

VALIS is both divine and system-like: vast, active, living, intelligent. The name itself fuses theology with cybernetic language.

This makes the novel a strange companion to [[Machine as God]]. Unlike dystopian machine-gods, VALIS may be salvific, but its form still blurs divine agency and technological system.

## Gnostic Hope and Paranoia

The novel never lets revelation become simple certainty. Fat may be mad, enlightened, deceived, or all three. That instability is the point.

For the graph, *VALIS* deepens the Gnostic branch by asking whether awakening from false reality can itself become another labyrinth of interpretation.
