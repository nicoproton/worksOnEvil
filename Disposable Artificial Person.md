# Disposable Artificial Person

Disposable Artificial Person names stories in which a made being is treated as labor, property, weapon, experiment, or replaceable product, then becomes morally visible as a person. The drama turns on recognition: what does the creator, owner, state, or hunter owe to a being made for use?

This bridge belongs under [[Manufactured Humanity]], but it is more precise than the parent concept because it focuses on the ethical reversal from instrument to neighbor.

Use this link when the made being's artificial origin is used to deny obligation, rights, kinship, pity, or moral recognition.

*[[R.U.R.]]* gives the motif its industrial form: artificial biological workers are manufactured to serve humanity, then inherit the consequences of being made as a class of tools.

*[[Blade Runner]]* and *[[Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep]]* both center made humanoids who are hunted, retired, or tested through uncertain human boundaries. Their artificiality becomes the excuse for disposal, even when they suffer, desire, remember, or respond.

*[[Metropolis (Tezuka)]]* and *[[Ghost in the Shell]]* shift the question toward artificial children, state-created intelligence, and emergent personhood that exceeds its intended function.

*[[Digimon (Franchise)]]* is a qualified case. Digimon are not manufactured humans, but the stronger entries ask whether beings first approached as data, toys, programs, or anomalies can become morally recognizable persons.
