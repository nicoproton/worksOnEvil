# Atheistic Communism

Atheistic Communism names the Catholic diagnosis of communism as a materialist, anti-religious, and revolutionary system that promises earthly salvation while denying God, the spiritual soul, natural rights, private property, family authority, and the final end of the person.

This bridge belongs under [[Life Redesigned by Ideology]] when society is treated as material for total reconstruction through class struggle, collective ownership, state power, and revolutionary doctrine. It also belongs near [[False Salvation Through Systems]] because the system offers a substitute redemption: justice, equality, fraternity, and a classless society, but at the price of the goods that make human life fully human.

Use this link when:

- materialism becomes a total account of man, society, history, and morality;
- class struggle is treated as the engine of history and social redemption;
- family, education, property, religion, and moral law are subordinated to the collectivity;
- political power claims the right to drag persons into a planned social whole;
- an earthly paradise is promised by denying man's supernatural end.

*[[Divini Redemptoris]]* is the main encyclical anchor. Pius XI describes atheistic communism as a false messianic system: it exploits real injustice, promises liberation, and then reduces the person to a function of matter, class, production, and collective power.

*[[Spe Salvi]]* gives a later theological reading of Marx's error. Benedict XVI argues that Marx turns hope toward politics and scientifically conceived revolution, but forgets that man remains free, that freedom remains open to evil, and that man cannot be redeemed merely by changing external conditions.

This concept should not be used for every work that criticizes capitalism, defends labor, imagines communal life, or uses socialist language. It belongs where the system is explicitly materialist, anti-religious, collectivist, and total in its claim over human life.
