# Dark Earth

***Dark Earth*** is a 1997 action-adventure game by Kalisto Entertainment and MicroProse. It takes place centuries after a cataclysm has covered the earth in darkness, leaving human beings to survive in rare sunlit city-states called Stallites. The game belongs in this vault because it combines fallen technological remains, light-centered civic religion, bodily contamination, and conspiracy inside a world where darkness behaves like a real hostile force.

## Sparta as a Citadel of Light

Sparta is one of the surviving Stallites, a city protected by sunlight and governed through the religion of Solaar. Its civic order depends on the light not only as resource, but as sacred center, boundary, and source of legitimacy.

This gives the game a careful relation to [[Cosmic Axis Under Siege]]. The Well or source of Light functions as the city's sustaining center, and the plot turns on the attempt to destroy that protection.

## The Great Cataclysm and the Darkened Earth

The world of *Dark Earth* is post-apocalyptic rather than simply medieval. Bunkers, technical remains, old records, weapons, and ruined infrastructures reveal that the present religious city exists after the collapse of a more technological age.

That makes the game a useful case of [[Fallen High Civilization]]. The old world is not the main object of the story, but its remnants shape the new order's ignorance, survival, and hidden possibilities.

## Arkhan's Contamination

Arkhan is contaminated by Shankr Archessence and begins to transform into a creature of darkness. The horror is bodily and personal: the outside darkness enters him and threatens his identity from within.

This links the game to [[Evil as Psychic Contagion]], though with an important qualification. The darkness is not merely the amplification of ordinary weakness; it behaves like an active substance or force that infects bodies and communities.

## Light and Darkness as Cosmic Forces

Unlike works shaped by privation of good, *Dark Earth* appears closer to [[Manichaeism and Zoroastrianism]] as a literary pattern. Evil is not framed only as the absence or corruption of good. Darkness seems to possess its own force, essence, contagion, and monstrous agency.

This makes the game useful as a contrast case inside the vault. It shows a dualist version of the light/dark conflict rather than the Tolkien-Augustine pattern where evil depends ontologically on the good it corrupts.

## Sunseers, Guardians, and Conspiracy

The attack on the Sunseers reveals hidden factions, compromised authorities, and deeper manipulation beneath the public religious order of Sparta. Arkhan's investigation moves through priests, guardians, sewers, political treachery, and concealed knowledge.

That gives the game a solid relation to [[Respectable Front]]. The threat is not only outside the city walls; the city's own offices and protectors are penetrated by secret allegiance and ambition.

## Old Bunkers and the Source of Light

Arkhan's search leads through old-world bunkers and hidden research toward the source of the Well of Light. Ancient knowledge becomes necessary for saving the city, but it is surrounded by partial memory, danger, and misused secrecy.

This places the game near [[Lost Technology as Temptation]], though in a less negative form than *[[Nausicaa of the Valley of the Wind (Manga)]]*. The old knowledge is dangerous, but it may also provide the path to reawaken the light that the city no longer understands.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Dark Earth* should be a modest but useful node. It connects most strongly to fallen civilization, sacred center under siege, contagion, and conspiracy. Its main caution is theological: the game should not be forced into privation of good, because its darkness is closer to a dualist hostile essence than to evil as lack.
