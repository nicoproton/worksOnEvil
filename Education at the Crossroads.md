# Education at the Crossroads

***Education at the Crossroads*** is Jacques Maritain's 1943 work on education, personhood, freedom, truth, and the ends of human formation. It belongs in this graph because it gives a Catholic personalist answer to administered education: education should form the person toward truth and freedom, not merely adapt him to society, production, or technique.

## Education for the Whole Person

Maritain's educational thought begins from anthropology. The student is not a future worker, citizen-function, psychological case, or social unit first. He is a person with intellect, freedom, moral life, social duties, and a spiritual end.

This gives the book a strong relation to [[Personalist Common Good]]. Education serves the common good by forming persons, not by manufacturing social components.

## Against Training Alone

The book belongs beside [[Administered Human Formation]] as a counterpoint. Maritain does not reject discipline, teachers, institutions, or civic formation, but he resists the reduction of education to social adaptation, useful skill, or state purpose.

This also connects it to [[Reason Ordered to Truth]]. Education must form intelligence toward truth, not only toward information, efficiency, or technique.

## Freedom and Discipline

Maritain's view is not permissive individualism. The person becomes free through formation, discipline, truth, and moral development.

That places the book near [[Liberty Detached from Truth]] by contrast. Education does not liberate by leaving the will alone; it liberates by ordering the person toward the good.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Education at the Crossroads* strengthens the graph's education branch by adding a Catholic personalist alternative to both institutional dependency and technocratic formation. It belongs beside Illich and Dawson without duplicating them: Illich critiques institutional monopoly, Dawson emphasizes cultural transmission, and Maritain emphasizes the person as the end of education.
