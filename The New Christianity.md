# The New Christianity

***The New Christianity*** (*Nouveau christianisme*, 1825) is Saint-Simon's late attempt to give industrial social reform a religious and moral center. It belongs in this graph because it shows modern reconstruction taking on Christian language while redirecting it toward social improvement, organization, and the elevation of the poorest.

The work should not be treated as simple atheistic technocracy. Its importance is stranger: religion is retained as moral energy, but the center of gravity moves toward humanity, social welfare, and organized progress.

## Social Religion

Saint-Simon's "new Christianity" emphasizes the moral duty to improve the condition of the poorest class. Religion is judged by its social effect.

This places the work near [[Religion of Humanity]], though it is not identical to Comte's later system. Saint-Simon keeps more Christian language, while Comte later builds a more explicit secular church of Humanity.

## Industrial Charity and Social Organization

The work links moral renewal to social organization. The poor are not to be helped only by private sentiment. Society itself should be arranged toward improvement.

This connects the work to [[Life Redesigned by Ideology]]. The moral doctrine does not remain inward devotion; it becomes a reason to reorganize social relations, authority, labor, and institutions.

## A Bridge to Secular Providence

The work also belongs near [[Progress as Secular Providence]]. The future social order begins to carry redemptive weight. Human suffering is to be answered by reform, organization, and historical improvement.

This is exactly the ambiguity that matters for the vault. The concern for the poor is real and morally serious. The danger appears when salvation is transferred into a designed social order administered by those who claim to know the future good.

## Why It Belongs Here

*The New Christianity* belongs because it shows the religious side of industrial modernity. It helps explain why later planning, architecture, and technocracy often present themselves not merely as efficient, but as humane, redemptive, and morally obligatory.
