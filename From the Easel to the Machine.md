# From the Easel to the Machine

***From the Easel to the Machine*** is Nikolai Tarabukin's 1923 Productivist text on the passage from autonomous painting to production. It belongs in this graph because its title states one of the key modernist transitions: art should leave the isolated artwork and enter the machine-age process of making.

This is not architecture in the narrow sense, but it is central to the ideology behind modernist design. It gives the Soviet form of the claim that art must become part of life.

## The End of the Easel

The easel represents autonomous art: framed, private, contemplative, separated from labor and social production.

Tarabukin's move toward the machine places the work under [[Art Into Life]]. Art should not remain an image of the world; it should become active within the productive world.

## Productivist Formation

The book belongs near [[Life Redesigned by Ideology]] because a revolutionary doctrine of society changes the meaning of art. The artist is not a private genius but a worker in the reorganization of material life.

This also connects to [[Administered Human Formation]] indirectly. Productivist art helps shape the objects, images, environments, and habits through which new socialist life is to appear.

## Neighbor to Constructivist Architecture

The work belongs beside *[[Style and Epoch]]* and *[[Russia - An Architecture for World Revolution]]*. Ginzburg applies epoch, labor, machine, and construction to architecture. Lissitzky presents Soviet architecture internationally. Tarabukin gives the art-theoretical crossing from picture to production.

## Why It Belongs Here

*From the Easel to the Machine* belongs because it gives the graph a concise formula for the modernist shift from art as representation to art as productive force. It helps connect Soviet art theory, architecture, design, propaganda, and the dream of a new collective life.
