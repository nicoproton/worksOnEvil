# Perfect Blue

Satoshi Kon's *Perfect Blue* is a 1997 animated film about Mima Kirigoe, a pop idol who leaves her group to become an actress and is drawn into stalking, online impersonation, sexualized performance, media pressure, and a breakdown in the boundary between public image and private self.

The film belongs in the graph because its horror is not only the obsessed fan. The fan, the manager, the website, the camera, the idol industry, and Mima's acting roles all participate in a system where the image of a person can become more socially powerful than the person herself.

## Idol Image as Second Self

Mima's idol persona does not disappear when she leaves the group. It continues as expectation, fan memory, commodity, fantasy, and accusation. The image becomes a rival self that claims to be more authentic than the living woman who wants to change.

This makes the film a strong case of [[Double and Divided Self]]. The double is not merely hallucinated; it is socially produced by media, fandom, performance, and commercial memory.

## The Person Inside the Media Image

The website "Mima's Room" is frightening because it describes Mima's life with an intimacy that feels stolen. Her private experience is re-presented to her as if the mediated version knows her better than she knows herself.

This connects the film to [[Mediated Reality]]. Mima does not only suffer from false stories about herself; she must live inside a media environment where others' images of her reshape the practical conditions of identity.

## Performance, Violation, and Self-Enclosure

Mima's acting career pushes her into roles and scenes that blur professional performance with personal violation. Her body and image become available for reinvention, sale, and interpretation by others.

This gives the film a relation to [[Aesthetic Self-Enclosure]], though the enclosure is not self-chosen decadence. It is the entertainment image-world enclosing a performer inside what can be watched, desired, scandalized, and consumed.

## Fandom as Dehumanizing Devotion

The fan's obsession matters because it shows love for an image becoming hatred of the person. He does not love Mima as a free being; he loves the idol-image he believes she owes him.

This keeps the film from being reduced to a stalker thriller. Fan violence is the exposed nerve of a larger problem: media devotion can become possessive when the image is treated as more real than the person.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Perfect Blue* belongs beside *[[Serial Experiments Lain]]*, *[[American Psycho]]*, and *[[Helter Skelter (Okazaki)]]*. It shows personhood split by image, performance, celebrity, voyeurism, and the demand that a public self remain consumable.
