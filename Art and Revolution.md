# Art and Revolution

***Art and Revolution*** is Richard Wagner's 1849 essay arguing that modern society has degraded art into commodity, fashion, and entertainment, while true art once belonged to communal religious life. It belongs in this graph because Wagner imagines revolution not only as political change, but as the recovery of a sacred communal art.

## Art After the Fall of Community

Wagner's argument begins from cultural fragmentation. In his account, Greek tragedy united music, poetry, dance, myth, religion, and civic life, while modern commercial society separates the arts and turns them into private consumption.

This places the essay near [[Hollowed Order]]. The forms of art remain, but the communal and sacred good that should animate them has been drained by modern fragmentation.

## Revolution as Aesthetic Renewal

The revolution Wagner imagines is not merely constitutional or economic. It should make possible a new public art that gathers the people into shared mythic feeling.

This gives the work a relation to [[Progress as Secular Providence]]. The future is imagined as a renewal of humanity through transformed social and artistic conditions.

## Art as Substitute Sacred Order

The essay is especially useful because art begins to take on religious weight. Wagner does not simply abolish the sacred; he relocates communal reverence into mythic aesthetic experience.

This places the work near [[Religion of Humanity]] cautiously. Wagner is not Comte, but both show modernity rebuilding sacred forms around human culture, collective feeling, and this-worldly transformation.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Art and Revolution* belongs because it connects Romantic radicalism to aesthetic formation. The revolutionary future is not only legislated or reasoned into being; it is sung, staged, mythologized, and felt.
