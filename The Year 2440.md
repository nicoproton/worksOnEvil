# The Year 2440

***The Year 2440*** (*L'An 2440, rêve s'il en fut jamais*) is Louis-Sebastien Mercier's 1771 future-set utopian novel. After a discussion about the injustices of Paris, the narrator falls asleep and awakens centuries later in a reformed Paris. The book is important because it shifts utopia from a distant island to historical time: the future becomes the place from which the present can be judged.

## The Future as Tribunal

Mercier's future Paris is not merely a fantasy elsewhere. It is Paris after reform, memory, moral correction, and civic improvement. The device is simple but powerful: the present is made strange by being viewed from a future that has outgrown its abuses.

This makes the novel a strong fictional case of [[Progress as Secular Providence]]. History itself becomes the road to judgment and renewal. The future reveals what the present ought to become.

Unlike later technological dystopias, Mercier's future is not defined by machines. Its force is moral, civic, educational, and social. The future is cleaner, more rational, more humane, and more legible.

## Enlightened Reform

The novel belongs to the pre-Revolutionary imagination of reform. It criticizes inequality, corruption, luxury, arbitrary power, and social disorder by showing a Paris where these have been corrected or transformed.

This gives it a distant ancestry relation to [[Scientific World State]], though the connection should be cautious. Mercier does not imagine a modern technocratic apparatus. He imagines an enlightened civic future where social improvement has become visible in institutions, manners, and urban order.

The stronger link is to the moral imagination of progress: present suffering is answered by a future in which reason and reform have become historical reality.

## Utopia Without Escape From History

Older utopias often travel to another land. Mercier travels forward in time. That change matters for the graph because it makes political hope historical rather than merely spatial.

The book therefore sits between *[[The Republic]]*, *[[The City of the Sun]]*, and later works like Wells's *[[A Modern Utopia]]*. It does not yet produce the full modern scientific world state, but it helps invent the expectation that the future can be designed, awaited, and used as judgment on the present.

## Why It Belongs Here

*The Year 2440* belongs because it gives the graph a pre-Revolutionary progress-fiction node. It is not dark enough to be a dystopia, and that is exactly why it is useful. The graph needs to remember that progress begins as hope, moral critique, and imaginative reform before later works expose its possible capture by systems.
