# Augustine of Hippo

**Augustine of Hippo** matters to this vault as a conceptual source for evil as privation, disordered love, and the political imagination of two cities. His value here is not biographical. He gives language for understanding why many works in the graph present evil as parasitic, hollow, or misdirected rather than as a creative power equal to the good.

## Evil as Privation

Augustine's account of evil rejects the idea that evil is a substance or rival principle. Evil is a corruption, lack, or disorder in something that is otherwise good insofar as it exists. This is the root of [[Privatio Boni]].

That framework helps explain why many villains and systems in the vault can organize, imitate, drain, or distort, but cannot truly create. Their power depends on some prior good: life, language, community, technology, beauty, or desire.

## Disordered Love

Augustine's moral psychology centers on love. Human beings are not only thinking creatures; they are loving creatures. Sin is not merely ignorance but misdirected love: loving lesser goods as ultimate, or loving the self in a way that severs it from God and neighbor.

A false system often succeeds by promising a real good, such as peace, unity, knowledge, or immortality, but ordering that good wrongly until it becomes destructive.

## The Two Cities

In *The City of God*, Augustine distinguishes the earthly city and the heavenly city by their loves: the earthly city is formed by self-love to the contempt of God, while the heavenly city is formed by love of God to the contempt of self.

For the graph, the two cities are useful as a way to think about political and institutional order. A city is not defined only by laws or architecture, but by the shared love that binds it. This gives theological depth to [[Entropy and the Binding Force]]: communities endure or decay according to what they finally love.

## Against Simple Dualism

Augustine's rejection of Manichaeism is important for distinguishing privation of good from cosmic dualism. Evil is not a second eternal principle opposed to God. It is a disorder within created being.

This matters when comparing works like *Terranigma*, which uses light/dark cosmic dualism, with works like *Paradise Lost*, where evil is parasitic rebellion inside a created order.
