# Stolen Life-Force Technology

Stolen Life-Force Technology names the motif in which technical power is produced by extracting vitality, spirit, magic, ecological energy, or sacred force from living beings or the world itself. The technology appears productive, but its productivity depends on draining a prior living order.

This concept often sits between [[Technological Dystopia]] and [[Privatio Boni]], but the second link should be used carefully. It fits privation when the machine or empire seems to create power but is actually converting a prior living good into fuel.

If the work treats the extracted force as part of a dualist cosmic struggle or a neutral resource economy, the privation link may not fit.

Use this link when a work's technology depends on extraction from persons, spirits, planets, gods, or ecosystems rather than neutral invention.

Tolkien's Silmarils are not technology in this sense, but *[[The Silmarillion]]* provides an important contrast: Morgoth cannot make the light of the Trees, only steal and imprison it.

When extraction becomes a social order rather than a machine, use [[Blood Economy]]. Vampire works often literalize life-force theft as blood, steam, youth, or psychic vitality.
