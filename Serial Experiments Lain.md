# Serial Experiments Lain

***Serial Experiments Lain*** is a 1998 anime series about Lain Iwakura, a withdrawn schoolgirl drawn into the Wired, a communication network that increasingly blurs with reality, memory, identity, and divinity. It is one of the graph's strongest works for technological transcendence as both revelation and threat.

## The Email From the Dead

The series begins after Chisa Yomoda's suicide, when classmates receive emails from her claiming she is not dead but has abandoned her body and entered the Wired. Lain's first contact with the network is therefore already spiritualized: death, communication, and disembodiment arrive together.

This makes the series a strong case for [[Artificial Immortality]]. Survival in the network appears possible, but only by loosening or abandoning embodied life.

## The Wired

The Wired begins as an internet-like communication network, but the series treats it as something much larger: a plane where memory, identity, unconscious communication, and reality itself begin to interpenetrate.

This makes *Lain* central to [[Simulated Reality]]. The problem is not only whether the world is digital, but whether reality is still distinguishable from the social and psychic network that interprets it.

## Lain as Node and Person

Lain's identity fragments across school, home, clubs, rumors, and the Wired. Multiple Lains seem to exist, each produced by different perceptions and systems of relation.

That gives the series a subtle link to [[Forced Human Completion]]. Lain is pressured to become more than a person: a node, goddess, protocol, collective function, or answer to the loneliness of the network.

## God in the Wired

The claim that "God is here" in the Wired gives the series its relation to [[Machine as God]]. The network does not merely host communication; it begins to demand metaphysical interpretation.

The series also resonates with [[Gnosticism and its Critics]]. Ordinary reality becomes unstable, hidden knowledge proliferates, and salvation seems to require awakening from the world as commonly perceived.

## Erasure and Mercy

Lain's final act involves altering memory and removing herself from ordinary relation. The ending is tender and terrifying: she saves others by becoming absent from them.

This places the series near [[Entropy and the Binding Force]]. Connection is the promised cure for isolation, but total connection risks erasing the very separateness that makes love meaningful.
