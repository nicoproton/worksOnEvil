# Living Tradition

Living Tradition names the Catholic pattern in which tradition is not a museum of old opinions, but the living transmission of revelation, worship, doctrine, interpretation, and ecclesial life. It is stable without being dead, and living without becoming self-invention.

The word "living" must be used in a strict sense. Tradition is living because the same apostolic deposit is handed on, contemplated, guarded, taught, and more deeply understood in the Church. It is not living because doctrine mutates into a different meaning. If "living tradition" means that later historical consciousness can reverse, replace, or judge revealed truth, the term has become [[Dogmatic Relativism]] or [[Theological Modernism]].

This bridge belongs near [[Theological Modernism]], [[Revelation Under Critical Reduction]], [[Spiritual Sense of Scripture]], and [[Ecclesial Communion]]. It is useful when the graph needs to distinguish tradition from both antiquarian fixity and modernist reinvention.

Use this link when:

- tradition transmits revelation through Church, liturgy, doctrine, Fathers, Scripture, and worship;
- development is possible but does not make the present age sovereign over truth;
- inherited forms are living because they carry a real good, not because they are old;
- Catholic renewal returns to sources without using the sources against the Church's living authority.

Do not use this link when:

- "tradition" is treated as historical material available for reconstruction;
- development means contradiction of the same doctrine in the same sense;
- sources are used against the living magisterium as if scholarship were a higher judge;
- novelty is baptized as tradition because it answers a modern need.

Yves Congar's *[[Tradition and Traditions]]* is the central modern anchor. It studies tradition historically and theologically without reducing it to either static repetition or historical relativism.

*[[Dei Filius]]* gives the doctrinal boundary through Scripture and Tradition as the deposit of revelation.

Henri de Lubac's *[[Medieval Exegesis]]* gives a scriptural-neighboring form: tradition is an intelligence of Scripture, not merely an archive of interpretations.

This concept is subordinate to *[[Dei Filius]]*, *[[Providentissimus Deus]]*, *[[Pascendi Dominici Gregis]]*, *[[Humani Generis]]*, and [[Spiritual Sense of Scripture]]. Congar's account is useful only if "living" means faithful transmission of the same truth.

This concept should not be used for every inherited custom. It belongs where tradition as theological transmission is at stake.
