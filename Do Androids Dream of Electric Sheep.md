# Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep

***Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?*** is Philip K. Dick's 1968 novel set after World War Terminus, in a radioactive world where real animals are rare, artificial animals are common, and escaped androids are hunted by bounty killers. Although *Blade Runner* adapts the novel, the book adds major motifs the film mostly leaves aside: Mercerism, empathy boxes, artificial animals, mood organs, and extinction guilt.

## World Terminus and Extinction Guilt

The novel's Earth is damaged, depopulated, and haunted by animal extinction. Owning a real animal is both status symbol and moral sign, while electric animals imitate the care relationship that ecological catastrophe has made scarce.

This links the novel to [[Entropy and the Binding Force]]. Care for animals becomes a fragile attempt to preserve relation in a dying world.

## Androids and the Empathy Boundary

Androids are physically human-like but are distinguished by supposed deficits of empathy. Deckard's work depends on the Voigt-Kampff test, which tries to locate humanity through affective response.

This makes the novel a major node for [[Manufactured Humanity]] and [[Empathy Technology]]. The question is not only whether androids are persons, but whether humans remain human when they can kill beings who look, speak, and suffer like them.

## Mercerism

Mercerism is a shared religion mediated through empathy boxes. Users fuse with Wilbur Mercer and with one another, participating in collective suffering and endurance.

This gives the novel a complex relation to [[Simulated Reality]]. The experience may be technologically mediated and partly exposed as constructed, yet it still seems to carry real ethical force.

## Mood Organs

The Penfield mood organ allows users to dial emotional states. Feeling becomes programmable.

This connects the novel to [[Engineered Happiness]]. Unlike *Brave New World*, the control is domestic and personal rather than fully state-administered, but the underlying question is similar: what happens when mood is treated as a technical setting?

## Electric Animals

The electric sheep and electric toad are not mere fake objects. They reveal the novel's core ambiguity: artificial things can still become objects of care, shame, tenderness, and moral attention.

This is why the novel deserves its own note despite *Blade Runner*. Dick's version is less about noir identity and more about whether empathy can survive in a world where nearly every sign of life may be artificial.
