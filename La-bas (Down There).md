# La-bas (Down There)

***La-bas*** (*Là-bas*, often translated as *Down There*) is Joris-Karl Huysmans's 1891 novel about Durtal, a writer disgusted with modern vulgarity who investigates the medieval child-murderer Gilles de Rais and contemporary Satanism in Paris. It is one of the strongest bridges between the decadent branch and the occult branch of the graph.

## From Decadence to Satanism

Huysmans had already made decadent withdrawal central in *[[Against Nature (A Rebours)]]*. *La-bas* moves that disgust outward: modern life is not only ugly, trivial, and spiritually exhausted; it may be spiritually inverted.

The novel belongs to [[Beauty from Corruption]] because it continues the decadent fascination with corruption, but it also strains against mere aestheticism. Evil is not only a flavor of experience. It becomes a question about worship, sacrilege, history, and whether modern unbelief has left the soul undefended.

## Durtal's Historical Mirror

Durtal's research into Gilles de Rais gives the novel a historical double structure. Medieval atrocity is not left in the past as a safe curiosity. It becomes a mirror for modern occult revival.

This makes the novel useful beside [[Hidden Correspondence System]]. Durtal reads history, symbols, rumors, ritual, and personalities as signs of a hidden spiritual continuity. The modern city is not transparent; it contains concealed channels back to older forms of evil.

## The Black Mass and Inverted Worship

The Satanic material makes *La-bas* more than a decadent mood-piece. Its occultism is organized around inversion: sacrament turned into sacrilege, prayer turned into curse, desire turned into ritual, and Catholic form used against Catholic meaning.

That places the novel near [[Ritual Technology]] only by analogy. There is no machine apparatus here. The sharper connection is ritual as a mechanism of spiritual inversion. It also belongs near [[Demonic Conspiracy]] as a boundary case: the novel imagines hidden Satanic networks, but its emphasis is investigative and decadent rather than a technocratic front like the N.I.C.E. in *[[That Hideous Strength]]*.

## Catholic Hunger Inside Decadence

The most important thing for this graph is that *La-bas* does not simply enjoy Satanism as exotic decor. The attraction to evil is bound to a hunger for real spiritual order. Huysmans's later movement toward Catholicism is already foreshadowed by the novel's dissatisfaction with aesthetic perversity alone.

This gives the book a strong relation to [[Privatio Boni]] in argument. Evil appears glamorous, elaborate, and historically deep, yet it remains dependent on the forms it inverts. Satanism needs the Mass in order to parody it.

That later movement becomes explicit in *[[En route]]*, *[[La Cathedrale]]*, and *[[L'Oblat]]*. Together they clarify that *La-bas* is not the final word of the Durtal arc. It is the descent that makes conversion necessary.

## Why It Belongs Here

*La-bas* belongs because it shows decadence discovering that aesthetic evil is not enough. The path from artificial sensation to occult inversion helps explain the graph's broader concern: modernity may reject transcendence in public while secretly reproducing inverted liturgies of power, appetite, and anti-creation.
