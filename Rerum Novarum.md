# Rerum Novarum

***Rerum Novarum*** is Pope Leo XIII's 1891 encyclical "On Capital and Labor." It belongs in this graph because it extends the Catholic branch from political order and liberty into the industrial social question: how should labor, property, wages, family, state action, and worker associations be judged once economic life has been unsettled by modern industry?

This note uses the official English text published by the Vatican.

## The Social Question After the Political Question

Leo presents the labor question as a new practical crisis following the age of political revolution. Industry has expanded, science has transformed production, older guild protections have disappeared, wealth has concentrated, and many workers have become isolated before employers and unchecked competition.

This makes the encyclical the main work note for [[Economic Order Under Moral Law]]. The question is not only how to distribute goods. It is whether economic life can be understood apart from moral law, religion, family, and the spiritual end of the person.

In graph terms, *Rerum Novarum* follows naturally after *[[Immortale Dei]]* and *[[Libertas]]*. Leo has already argued that civil society and liberty must be ordered to truth. Now he applies that logic to labor and property.

*[[Quadragesimo Anno]]* later develops this social teaching into a fuller account of reconstruction, [[Subsidiarity]], economic concentration, and the dangers of both socialism and economic individualism.

## Socialism as the Wrong Remedy

Leo begins by rejecting socialism's proposed abolition or collectivization of private property. His objection is not simply that socialism is inefficient. He argues that it injures the worker, violates justice, distorts the role of the state, and attacks the family.

The worker labors partly in order to acquire and dispose of property. If his savings are converted into land, tools, or household stability, those things are not alien to his labor; they are his labor under another form. To abolish private ownership is therefore to strike at the worker's hope of securing a more stable life.

This places the encyclical in argument with [[Life Redesigned by Ideology]]. Leo does not deny the misery of industrial workers. He denies that misery should be answered by making property, family, and labor into material for a total social reconstruction administered from above.

## Property and the Family Before the State

One of the strongest themes is that the family is a true society older than the state. Marriage, parental authority, household provision, inheritance, and the father's duty to provide are not creations of civil law.

Private property becomes more than an individual possession here. It belongs to the family's ability to endure, provide, educate, and transmit life across time. That is why Leo treats state absorption of household authority as an attack on natural justice.

This connects carefully to [[Secular State Supremacy]]. The state has real duties, but it does not create the family and must not absorb it. The family enters civil society as a prior natural society, not as a state-made administrative unit.

## Not Laissez-Faire

The encyclical is equally clear that private property does not justify economic cruelty. Leo condemns employers who treat workers as bondsmen, grind them down for gain, defraud them of wages, overwork them, expose them to corrupting conditions, or ignore their religious and family duties.

This is why *Rerum Novarum* should not be filed as a simple defense of capitalism. It defends ownership, but it subordinates ownership to justice, charity, the common good, and man's supernatural end.

The distinction is one of the note's greatest graph values. Leo refuses the false choice between socialist absorption and market autonomy. Economic life must be free enough for property, family, and association, but governed enough by moral law that the poor are not abandoned to power.

## Work Is Personal and Necessary

Leo's account of wages turns on two characters of labor. Labor is personal because the worker's effort belongs to him; it is necessary because he must work to live. A wage contract therefore cannot be judged by consent alone if necessity forces the worker to accept terms that do not support life.

This gives the encyclical a strong link to [[Liberty Detached from Truth]]. A formally free contract can conceal coercion when one party's poverty leaves him no real room to refuse. The mere appearance of consent does not exhaust justice.

That also makes the note a useful counterpoint to modern systems that treat the person as a replaceable economic component. Work belongs to a human being with a soul, family, limits, duties to God, and a need for rest.

## Rest, Religion, and the Worker as Soul

Leo insists that workers need time for religious duties, rest, family life, and bodily recovery. Sunday rest is not mere idleness; it is ordered toward worship and the soul's higher life.

This is where *Rerum Novarum* strongly resists the total technical environment. Industrial labor is not allowed to consume the whole person. The worker is not merely productive capacity. He is ordered toward God, and no one may demand conditions that make him surrender the duties he owes to God and to himself.

That makes the encyclical a Catholic social counterpart to the graph's anti-mechanistic works. The issue is not only exploitation. It is the reduction of human life to productive force.

## The State Must Help Without Absorbing

Leo gives the state a real role. Public authority should protect property, prevent injustice, watch over the poor, regulate grave abuses, protect health and morals, prevent conflicts where possible, and secure conditions under which workers can live with dignity.

But the same encyclical insists on limits. The state must not absorb the individual, the family, or private associations. Law should intervene according to the nature of the evil being remedied, and not go further than the remedy requires.

This belongs squarely inside [[Christian Civil Order]]. The state serves the common good under natural and divine law. It is neither absent nor absolute.

## Associations Between Individual and State

The last major remedy is association. Leo praises mutual aid, worker organizations, Catholic societies, and adapted forms of older guild life. These lesser societies arise from natural sociability and should be protected rather than crushed by the state.

This gives the encyclical one of its most important anti-totalizing elements. Between isolated individual and centralized state there must be real communities: family, Church, unions, mutual aid societies, religious associations, and local forms of cooperation.

This is why *Rerum Novarum* can help the graph distinguish social order from social engineering. The remedy for atomized labor is not only state administration, but the restoration and multiplication of mediating bodies.

## Why It Matters Here

*Rerum Novarum* adds the industrial-economic branch to the encyclical map:

- it rejects socialism's abolition of private property;
- it rejects employer domination and market cruelty;
- it treats the family as prior to the state;
- it treats labor as personal, necessary, embodied, and spiritual;
- it allows state intervention for justice while limiting state absorption;
- it defends associations as real societies between individual and state.

Its graph role is to show that Catholic resistance to modernity is not simply negative. The encyclical answers a real wound of industrial society, but refuses both total redesign and economic indifference. The worker must not be sacrificed to the market, the state, or the revolutionary scheme.
