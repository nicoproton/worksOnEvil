# Corporate Sovereignty

Corporate Sovereignty names worlds where corporations do not merely sell products inside a political order, but take on state-like powers: policing, war, law, urban planning, bodily ownership, medical control, data control, and the definition of whose life counts.

This bridge belongs near [[Domination Through the Machine]], [[Alienating City]], and [[Manufactured Humanity]]. It is strongest when corporate power becomes the environment in which persons live, rather than one villainous company doing one bad thing.

Reinhold Martin's *[[The Organizational Complex]]* gives the non-fiction prehistory of this pattern. It does not describe cyberpunk corporate sovereignty, but it shows corporate architecture, media, computers, and organizational thought beginning to shape the postwar landscape as a system of work, image, and control.

Use this link when:

- corporations own bodies, labor, memory, weapons, city space, or identity;
- private security, military force, or police power becomes corporate;
- citizenship is displaced by contract, employment, debt, access, or property status;
- persons are valued primarily as assets, products, data, or disposable costs.

*[[Blade Runner]]* gives the corporate-creation form: Tyrell manufactures persons, memories, labor, and death-limits. *[[RoboCop]]* gives the municipal-police form: OCP wants to own law enforcement, urban redevelopment, and Murphy's rebuilt body. *[[Stand on Zanzibar]]* gives the managed-development form, where General Technics acts on nations, infrastructure, and futures through planning and expertise. *[[Synners]]* gives the neural-entertainment form, where direct access to creativity and perception becomes a media market. *[[Feed]]* gives the consumer-formation form, where implanted advertising helps shape thought and adolescence. *[[Kaiba]]* gives the memory-market form, where stored identity and body exchange create a class order around artificial survival. *[[He, She and It]]* gives the enclave form, where corporate worlds compete with fragile free communities. *[[Hardwired]]* gives the orbital-corporate form, where corporations function as post-national military and economic powers. *[[Cyberpunk 2020]]* turns this into a playable world-system of megacorporations, private violence, cyberware, and street survival.

This concept should not be used for every story with a corporation. It matters when corporate power becomes sovereign enough to replace political, familial, or moral obligations with ownership, contract, and utility.
