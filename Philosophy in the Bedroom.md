# Philosophy in the Bedroom

***Philosophy in the Bedroom*** (*La philosophie dans le boudoir*) is the Marquis de Sade's 1795 libertine dialogue in which sexual initiation, anti-Christian polemic, and revolutionary politics are fused into a program of transgression. It is one of the graph's clearest examples of ideology serving corruption rather than merely decorating it.

## Instruction as Corruption

The work is structured as education. Eugénie is not simply tempted into isolated vice; she is instructed into a worldview. The bedroom becomes a classroom where desire is rationalized, modesty is treated as superstition, and cruelty is converted into doctrine.

This makes the text a strong case of [[Corrupting Guide]]. The guide does not only seduce. The guide teaches a language in which corruption can call itself nature, liberty, and philosophy.

## Libertinism as Political Program

The text belongs centrally to [[Libertine Rationalism]]. Its scandal is not only erotic content. It turns libertinism into a political and metaphysical argument: if the Revolution has overthrown throne and altar, moral restraint must also be abolished.

This gives the work a real relation to [[Secular Antichrist Utopia]], though as a negative and fragmentary precursor rather than a utopian system. The Christian order is not merely rejected; it is inverted into an anti-catechism of appetite and domination.

## Nature Without Goodness

Sadean nature is not Tolkien's created order or Aquinas's intelligible order toward the good. Nature is invoked as permission for violence, pleasure, and the stronger will.

This makes the work a dark branch of [[Weaponized Rationalism]]. Argument and naturalistic language become tools for stripping persons of moral protection.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Philosophy in the Bedroom* belongs because it shows one ideological root of the graph's later nightmare: the claim that liberation requires the abolition of inherited limits, and that once God, nature as order, and personhood are dismissed, the body becomes material for power.
