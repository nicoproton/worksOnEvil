# The Shape of Things to Come

***The Shape of Things to Come*** is H. G. Wells's 1933 future history imagining global war, social breakdown, plague, reconstruction, and the emergence of a scientifically organized world civilization. It belongs in this graph because catastrophe becomes the threshold through which expert planners claim authority over the future human order.

## Catastrophe as Gateway

The work does not treat world war as a final end. War and breakdown become the conditions under which older political forms lose legitimacy and a new world order can emerge.

This connects the book to [[Managed Crisis]]. The crisis is real, but its narrative function is to open the way for larger coordination, expert rule, and the reorganization of human affairs.

## Scientific Reconstruction

Wells's future is rebuilt by a modernizing, scientifically minded elite. The answer to catastrophe is not penitence, inherited local order, or spiritual conversion, but rational planning at civilizational scale.

This gives the work a strong relation to [[Scientific World State]]. It is one of Wells's most direct fictional attempts to imagine the passage from world disaster to planned global order.

## Salvation by Planning

The book's importance for the Fallout-adjacent branch is that the old world does not simply fall. Its technical and administrative mentality survives as the proposed cure for the fall.

That places the work near [[Sterile Scientific Salvation]]. Wells's version is comparatively respectable and aspirational, not grotesque like later bunker laboratories. But it establishes the pattern: scientific administration becomes the answer to civilizational self-destruction.

## Future Humanity

The reconstructed world requires new loyalties, education, social discipline, and a changed sense of historical belonging. The future is not only built; it forms the people who will inhabit it.

This connects the work to [[Administered Human Formation]] and [[World-Consciousness Formation]]. Human beings must be educated into the world order that claims to save them.

## Why It Belongs Here

*The Shape of Things to Come* belongs because it gives the dignified ancestor of a darker post-apocalyptic pattern. Later works will imagine laboratories, AIs, and military bases continuing the same dream after its humane confidence has curdled.
