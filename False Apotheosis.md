# False Apotheosis

False Apotheosis names the false promise that selected human beings will be elevated beyond ordinary creaturely life: immortal, post-organic, godlike, purified, or fit to rule the future. The promised ascent is a trap. The power offering elevation does not intend to fulfill human life, but to use the aspirants as material for possession, sterility, replacement, or final disposal.

This bridge belongs near [[Artificial Immortality]], [[Designed Successor Humanity]], [[Demonic Conspiracy]], and [[Death Refused as Gift]]. It is strongest when the promise of becoming more than human hides a deeper will to make humanity less than human.

Use this link when:

- an elite is promised survival, godhood, posthuman transformation, or spiritual promotion;
- the same project treats ordinary humans as obsolete, impure, expendable, or transitional;
- immortality or transcendence is purchased by rejecting embodiment, relation, mortality, fertility, or moral law;
- the hidden power benefits by consuming, possessing, replacing, or discarding the would-be gods.

*[[That Hideous Strength]]* is the central case. The N.I.C.E. imagines a selected post-organic future and treats the "Macrobes" as higher life, but the preserved Head already shows the truth: the supposed immortal intelligence is a medium used by fallen powers. The elite's promised apotheosis is really a route toward anti-incarnational possession and a dead world.

The fall of Numenor in *[[The Silmarillion]]* gives a mythic theological analogue. Sauron turns fear of death into the promise that men can seize deathlessness by rebellion, but the promised elevation leads to worship, catastrophe, and the destruction of the civilization that believed him.

*[[Terranigma]]* gives a qualified technocratic-game analogue through Beruga. His preserved remnant and immortality project promise survival beyond ordinary death, but the project is captured by Dark Gaia's anti-life design. The game should not be flattened into Lewis's Christian demonology: Beruga's evil lies in turning healing science into selection, stasis, and an unchanging world. *[[BLAME!]]* gives a cyberpunk limit case when a satanic-transhumanist desire for the next evolutionary stage helps unleash a machine-world in which ordinary humanity becomes illegitimate life.

*[[Camp Concentration]]* gives a secular enhancement form: intelligence is elevated through an imposed experiment that consumes the subject. *[[The Three Stigmata of Palmer Eldritch]]* gives a more metaphysical and commercial form, where expanded reality may become possession or absorption into Eldritch's presence.

This concept should not be used for every transhuman or technological-divinity story. *[[Deus Ex]]*, for example, asks whether merging with Helios could be benevolent machine rule; the game leaves the question politically and metaphysically ambiguous. Use [[Networked Divinity]] there unless the promised elevation is clearly exposed as a trap.
