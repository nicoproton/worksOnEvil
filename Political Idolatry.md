# Political Idolatry

Political Idolatry names the pattern in which a real political or communal good is raised above its proper order and treated as sacred in itself. Race, people, nation, state, leader, destiny, class, empire, or revolution may become a rival absolute, demanding loyalty that belongs to God and judging religion by political myth.

This bridge belongs near [[Secular State Supremacy]], but it is not identical with it. Secular State Supremacy emphasizes civil power claiming final jurisdiction over religion, family, education, and moral law. Political Idolatry emphasizes sacralization: the political order does not merely govern; it receives religious intensity.

Use this link when:

- race, people, nation, state, class, or leader is raised to an idolatrous level;
- religion is tolerated only if it serves a national, racial, or political myth;
- loyalty to the state is treated as higher than loyalty to Christ, Church, conscience, or divine law;
- a political movement creates substitutes for revelation, worship, moral law, or salvation history;
- human community is made sacred by denying the Creator who gives it its real place.

*[[Mit Brennender Sorge]]* is the central encyclical anchor. Pius XI condemns the divinization of race, people, state, and political power, especially when these are used to distort Christianity into a national or racial religion.

[[Religion of Humanity]] is a neighboring but different pattern. It sacralizes humanity as a universal collective object. Political Idolatry more often sacralizes a particular people, race, state, class, or regime.

[[Atheistic Communism]] is also a neighbor when class and revolution take on false-messianic force. The distinction is useful: atheistic communism denies God through materialist class redemption; political idolatry may keep religious language while bending it around nation, blood, state, or leader.

This bridge should not be used for every patriotic, civic, or communal work. It belongs where a relative political good is given absolute, sacred, or salvific weight.
