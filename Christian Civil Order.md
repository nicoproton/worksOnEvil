# Christian Civil Order

Christian Civil Order names the Catholic political pattern in which civil society is real, natural, and necessary, but not self-grounding. Public authority exists for the common good, comes ultimately from God, is bound by natural and divine law, and must not treat religion, family, education, or moral truth as merely civil inventions.

This bridge is the positive counterpart to [[Secular State Supremacy]]. It does not mean that every state must have the same regime, constitution, or administrative form. It means that civil power is legitimate only when it remains ordered to truth, justice, the common good, and man's final end.

Use this link when:

- political authority is defended as natural and God-given rather than merely contractual or self-created by the people;
- civil law is judged by truth, justice, natural law, and the common good;
- Church and state are distinguished without making the Church subordinate to the state;
- family, education, and religion are treated as goods the state must respect rather than fabricate;
- public liberty is praised only when ordered to truth and virtue.

*[[Immortale Dei]]* is the main encyclical anchor. Leo XIII gives a constructive account of civil society, public authority, Church-state distinction, ordered liberty, and Catholic political action.

*[[Libertas]]* gives the theory of freedom inside that order. Leo argues that public liberty is real only when civil law, education, speech, worship, and conscience remain ordered toward truth and the eternal law.

*[[Rerum Novarum]]* extends the same order into the social question through [[Economic Order Under Moral Law]]. Leo defends labor, property, family, just wages, worker associations, and state action under natural law and the common good.

*[[Quadragesimo Anno]]* deepens that social order through [[Subsidiarity]], insisting that higher authorities should help lower societies without absorbing their proper functions.

*[[Quanta Cura]]* and the *[[Syllabus of Errors]]* provide the negative map: they show what happens when naturalism, indifferentism, public opinion, and civil supremacy replace the public duties of society toward God.

[[Thomas Aquinas]] is conceptually important because the bridge depends on natural law, ordered ends, the common good, and reason as a servant of truth rather than power. The link should remain conceptual rather than a claim that every Catholic political document is simply Thomism.

This bridge should not be used for every conservative or anti-revolutionary text. It belongs where civil order is explicitly judged by divine and natural law rather than by technique, history, will, utility, or administrative power alone.
