# High-Rise

***High-Rise*** is J. G. Ballard's 1975 novel about a luxury apartment tower whose residents descend into tribal violence, class hostility, and enclosed social collapse. It belongs in this graph because modern architecture becomes a total social environment that releases rather than civilizes predation.

## The Tower as Complete Habitat

The high-rise contains apartments, services, social spaces, hierarchies, and technical systems. It is designed to be a self-contained environment for modern life.

This makes the novel a strong case of [[Total Technical Environment]]. The building is not only a setting; it is the world-condition the residents inhabit.

## Architecture and Social Regression

The tower's class divisions and technical failures become social accelerants. As order breaks down, residents do not simply return to nature. They form new patterns of territoriality, status, violence, and adaptation inside the building.

This connects the novel to [[Alienating City]]. The city is compressed into architecture: infrastructure, class, privacy, proximity, and hostility become one pressure field.

## Enclosed License

The building gradually becomes a world with its own permissions. What would be unthinkable outside becomes normal inside as the residents adapt to collapse.

This gives the novel a limited relation to the closed-world pattern. The high-rise is not Sade's sovereign estate, but it becomes an enclosed anti-world where moral witness and ordinary civic order thin out.

## Why It Belongs Here

*High-Rise* belongs because it bridges *[[Crash (Ballard)]]* and *[[American Psycho]]*. Ballard's modern environment does not only mediate desire through cars; it can build a whole vertical society where status, violence, and technical habitat become inseparable.
