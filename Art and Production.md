# Art and Production

***Art and Production*** is Boris Arvatov's 1926 statement of Soviet Productivist theory. It belongs in this graph because it gives one of the clearest ideological versions of art leaving autonomous culture and entering production, labor, objects, and collective life.

Arvatov's importance is that he radicalizes [[Art Into Life]]. Art should not merely represent the new society or decorate it. It should participate in producing the material and social forms of that society.

## Against Autonomous Art

The work rejects art as a separate realm of easel painting, private contemplation, or bourgeois cultural prestige. Art should be socially useful and integrated with production.

This places the book under [[Life Redesigned by Ideology]]. A political theory of labor and society tells art what it must become.

## Object, Labor, and Everyday Life

Productivism redirects artistic energy toward useful objects, industrial design, material culture, and the organization of everyday life.

This makes the work a strong neighbor to [[Social Condenser]]. Soviet architecture and Productivist art share a belief that material forms can help produce new socialist habits.

The relation to Bauhaus should be careful. Bauhaus also joins art, craft, and design, but Arvatov's framework is more explicitly revolutionary and production-centered.

## From Art to Construction

The work belongs beside *[[From the Easel to the Machine]]*. Tarabukin gives the dramatic formula of leaving the easel for machine production. Arvatov provides the broader social and theoretical argument for art as production.

Together they deepen the Soviet side of [[Art Into Life]] beyond architecture alone.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Art and Production* belongs because it shows how ideology can redirect art into the making of a new social world. It helps explain why architecture, design, propaganda, objects, and work could be treated as one revolutionary field.
