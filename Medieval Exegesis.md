# Medieval Exegesis

***Medieval Exegesis*** is Henri de Lubac's multi-volume study of the four senses of Scripture and the medieval tradition of spiritual interpretation. It belongs in this graph because it gives a constructive Catholic alternative to reductive criticism: Scripture can be studied historically without being flattened into a merely human text.

## The Fourfold Sense

De Lubac retrieves the older pattern of literal, allegorical, moral, and anagogical senses. The point is not arbitrary symbolism. The point is that Scripture is inspired and unified by God, ordered through Christ, the Church, moral conversion, and final destiny.

This makes the work central to [[Spiritual Sense of Scripture]].

## History Without Reduction

The work is important because it does not reject history. Medieval exegesis begins from the literal sense, but the literal sense does not exhaust divine meaning.

This makes the work a constructive neighbor of [[Revelation Under Critical Reduction]]. The answer to reductive criticism is not ignorance of history, but a fuller account of what kind of text Scripture is.

## Tradition as Intelligence

De Lubac shows that the Fathers and medieval interpreters were not simply naive readers before modern method. They worked inside a theological intelligence in which Scripture, liturgy, doctrine, and spiritual life belonged together.

This places the work near [[Theological Modernism]] by contrast. Modernist reduction often treats dogma and Scripture as historical products of religious consciousness. De Lubac retrieves a tradition in which history and divine meaning are not enemies.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Medieval Exegesis* belongs because the Catholic branch needs a positive account of interpretation. The graph already has warnings against rationalist reduction. De Lubac adds the richer alternative: Scripture is a real historical text whose deepest meaning is opened by Christ, the Church, and the spiritual senses.
