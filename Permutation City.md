# Permutation City

Greg Egan's *Permutation City* is a 1994 science-fiction novel about uploaded human minds, simulated worlds, artificial life, and the metaphysics of computation. It belongs in the graph because it takes the cyberpunk dream of disembodiment seriously enough to ask what personhood becomes if consciousness can run as software.

## Copies and the Question of Continuity

The novel's Copies are not merely backups or avatars. They raise the question of whether a computational process can continue the self, and whether that continuity remains human when detached from ordinary bodily life.

This makes the novel a major case of [[Disembodied Persistence]]. Survival is imagined through executable mind rather than through vulnerable embodied life.

## Consciousness as Executable Information

The novel also belongs to [[Inward Life as Data]]. In *Permutation City*, inward life is not only recorded; it is treated as the kind of thing that can be instantiated, paused, copied, slowed, simulated, and relocated.

This is deeper than gadget cyberpunk. The technology changes ontology: the person becomes a process whose relation to body, time, and world has to be rethought.

## Artificial Immortality and Its Cost

The promise of digital survival gives the novel a careful relation to [[Artificial Immortality]]. The attraction is obvious: death and bodily decay might be bypassed. But the cost is equally central: persistence may become isolation, abstraction, ownership, or a life whose conditions are no longer creaturely.

The link should not be flattened into simple anti-technology. Egan is philosophically serious. The graph value is the pressure the work places on the old question: whether immortality by technique can preserve the goods that make life human.

## Simulated Worlds and Artificial Life

The Autoverse and other simulated environments place the novel near [[Simulated Reality]], but the emphasis is more metaphysical than conspiratorial. The question is not only whether a world is false, but whether a simulated world can become real enough for its inhabitants.

That makes *Permutation City* a useful limit case for the graph. It pushes simulation beyond deception into creation, continuity, and artificial being.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Permutation City* belongs because it treats digital personhood as a radical change in the meaning of self, body, time, death, and world. It is not cyberpunk atmosphere; it is post-cyberpunk metaphysics.
