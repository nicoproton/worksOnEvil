# The New World Order (Wells)

***The New World Order*** is H. G. Wells's 1940 book arguing for a planned world order after the failures of war, nationalism, and existing international politics. Compared with *[[The Open Conspiracy]]*, it is less a manifesto of intellectual recruitment and more an argument for world government, rights, and rational reconstruction.

## World Government as Cure

Wells treats world government as the necessary answer to global war and political fragmentation. Peace cannot be secured, in his view, by old sovereign states competing inside the same international system.

This makes the book a strong case for [[Scientific World State]]. The proposed cure to catastrophe is not local virtue or inherited tradition, but institutional planetary organization.

## Rights and Planning

Wells is not simply calling for tyranny. He connects world order to human rights, law, and rational administration. That makes the book more interesting for the graph: the danger is not an openly evil system, but a system proposed as the only rational protector of humanity.

This links to [[False Salvation Through Systems]]. The book's promise is real peace and stability; the graph question is what happens when peace becomes dependent on total administrative redesign.

## Education for World Citizenship

Wells's world order requires new loyalties. People must be educated away from narrow nationalism and toward a planetary civic identity.

This connects the book to [[Engineered Consent]] and [[Language as Control System]]. A world state is not built only through law; it requires a new vocabulary of belonging.

## Wells's Utopian Continuity

The book continues themes already present in *A Modern Utopia* and *The Open Conspiracy*: elite organization, scientific planning, world citizenship, and the replacement of inherited order by designed order.

For the vault, the Wells cluster is valuable because it gives a real intellectual source for the world-state imagination behind many fictional systems.
