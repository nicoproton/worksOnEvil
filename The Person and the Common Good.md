# The Person and the Common Good

***The Person and the Common Good*** is Jacques Maritain's 1947 work of social philosophy on personhood, individuality, society, and the common good. It belongs in this graph because it gives a precise Catholic answer to two opposite reductions: man as isolated individual and man as raw material for the collective.

## Individuality and Personality

Maritain distinguishes individuality from personality. As an individual, man is part of the material and social order. As a person, he is a spiritual whole, ordered toward truth, freedom, love, and God.

This makes the book the central anchor for [[Personalist Common Good]]. The distinction lets Maritain affirm real social belonging without allowing the political whole to absorb the person.

## The Common Good Is Real

Maritain does not reduce society to a contract among private wills. The common good is a real good of the political community, and persons have duties toward it.

This places the book near [[Christian Civil Order]]. Civil order is not merely an arrangement for private preference; it is ordered toward justice, truth, moral life, and the flourishing of persons.

## The Person Is Not State Material

The book's importance for the graph is its resistance to total absorption. The person belongs to society, but not as a tool belongs to a machine or a cell belongs to an organism whose end overrides it.

This makes the book a counterpoint to [[Atheistic Communism]] and [[Administered Human Formation]]. The person may be formed by education and law, but he is not a product of the state, class, school, party, or expert system.

It also belongs near [[Subsidiarity]]. Lower communities and personal agency matter because the common good is not served by drawing all functions upward into a single administrative center.

## Why It Belongs Here

*The Person and the Common Good* is a strong addition because it gives the graph a Catholic personalist center. Many works in the vault show systems that either isolate the self or dissolve it into a collective. Maritain gives the metaphysical and political distinction needed to resist both errors.
