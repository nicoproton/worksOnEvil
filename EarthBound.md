# EarthBound

***EarthBound***, released in Japan as *Mother 2*, is a 1994 role-playing game by Shigesato Itoi and Ape/HAL Laboratory. It follows Ness, Paula, Jeff, and Poo as they travel through a comic, surreal, contemporary world under the influence of Giygas. Its strongest place in the graph is the final inversion of power: cosmic evil is defeated by prayer, memory, friendship, and the player's participation rather than by combat.

## Giygas as Lost Self

Giygas is no longer simply the wounded alien of *EarthBound Beginnings*. By the end of *EarthBound*, his evil power has become so vast that his mind and selfhood have collapsed. He is contained by the Devil's Machine, then released into incoherent psychic horror.

This gives the game one of the graph's clearest images of [[Privatio Boni]]. Evil expands as power but diminishes as personhood. The more absolute Giygas becomes, the less he is himself.

## Mani Mani and Psychic Contagion

The Evil Mani Mani statue amplifies greed, delusion, cultic obsession, and distortion. Its influence appears in Happy Happy Village, Moonside, and the corruption of people and places.

This makes the game a strong case of [[Evil as Psychic Contagion]]. Evil is not only external attack; it spreads by intensifying disordered desires already present in persons and communities.

## Moonside and Distorted Speech

Moonside is a warped world where yes and no are reversed, social logic breaks, and ordinary communication becomes unstable. It is not a full simulation, but a local collapse of shared reality.

This connects *EarthBound* to [[Language as Control System]]. The world becomes dangerous when words and meanings no longer bind persons to reality.

## Devil's Machine

The Devil's Machine contains and stabilizes Giygas. It is a technological shell around cosmic evil, a device that preserves power without restoring personhood.

The machine does not save Giygas; it prolongs and contains his privation.

## Prayer and the Player

In the final battle, Paula prays, and the prayer reaches friends across the world. Eventually it reaches beyond the game to the player. The victory is communal and participatory.

This is a major instance of [[Song and Prayer as Binding Force]] and [[Eucatastrophe]]. The final answer to Giygas is not a stronger weapon but relation: remembered persons, gathered prayer, and the refusal to let the children face evil alone.
