# Image-Generated Self

Image-Generated Self names the pattern in which an image, role, recording, portrait, performance, celebrity persona, or fantasy-version of the person begins to generate a rival self. The image no longer merely represents the person; it produces expectations, desires, guilt, doubles, persecutions, or alternate identities.

This bridge belongs between [[Mediated Reality]] and [[Double and Divided Self]]. It is more specific than general mediation because the image begins to act like a self. It is more specific than general doubling because the double is produced through representation.

## Use This Link When

- a portrait, screen image, recording, performance, idol persona, or public image becomes more socially or psychologically powerful than the person;
- the self tries to live inside an image and becomes divided by it;
- media, artifice, or performance produces a rival identity;
- the work shows the cost of mistaking an image of life for life itself.

## Strong Cases

*[[The Picture of Dorian Gray]]* gives the Gothic portrait form. The portrait receives the visible consequence that Dorian refuses, becoming a rival image of his hidden soul.

*[[Perfect Blue]]* gives the idol-media form. Mima's public image becomes a competing self produced by fandom, performance, commercial memory, and online impersonation.

*[[Helter Skelter (Okazaki)]]* gives the celebrity-body form. The body is remade for the image until the image-world consumes the person it displays.

*[[Mulholland Drive]]* gives the Hollywood dream form. Betty can be read as an image-self generated out of Diane's desire, grief, and failure, though the film should not be reduced to that reading alone.

*[[Lost Highway]]* gives the recorded-image form. Videotapes and identity fracture make the self seem watched, replaced, and rewritten.

*[[Inland Empire]]* gives the performance-maze form. Role, actress, character, screen, room, and identity begin to pass into one another.

## Neighboring Cases

*[[American Psycho]]* belongs nearby because Bateman's public self is made from status images, brands, routines, and media surfaces. The fit is looser than in *Perfect Blue* or *Mulholland Drive*, because the image is more social and commodity-based than a literal double.

[[Bovarysme]] is a literary ancestor. The desired image of life begins to govern ordinary reality, even before electronic media.

[[Semiotic Dislocation]] is a neighboring inward problem. The image-generated self is about a rival representation becoming powerful; semiotic dislocation is about the person being unable to find a truthful sign or role by which to understand himself.

## Why It Belongs Here

This bridge helps reduce graph noise. Instead of linking every mediated-identity work directly to every other one, the works can meet through the more precise pattern: the self made, pursued, or destroyed by its image.
