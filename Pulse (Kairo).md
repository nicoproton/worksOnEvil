# Pulse

Kiyoshi Kurosawa's *Pulse* (*Kairo*) is a 2001 horror film in which ghosts begin appearing through computers and internet connections as ordinary life in Tokyo empties into isolation, despair, and disappearance. Its power lies in treating the internet not as a monster-machine, but as a medium through which loneliness becomes metaphysical.

## The Internet as Failed Communion

The film's terror begins with connection that does not connect. Screens open onto presences, rooms, and messages, but the result is not relation. The network becomes a channel for contact without communion.

This connects *Pulse* to [[Mediated Reality]]. The screen does not simply show ghosts; it changes what presence means. The mediated encounter becomes more haunting than absence.

## Eternal Loneliness

The ghosts in *Pulse* are frightening because they suggest a condition worse than death: persistence without relation. Their presence does not restore the dead to community. It spreads the loneliness in which they are trapped.

This makes the film a strong case of [[Disembodied Persistence]]. It imagines survival or presence after bodily life as a curse when it lacks embodied relation, memory, and love.

## Loneliness as Contagion

The film's disappearances feel less like attacks by individual spirits than like an epidemic of despair. Loneliness spreads through rooms, screens, urban spaces, and the feeling that other people are unreachable.

That gives *Pulse* a strong relation to [[Evil as Psychic Contagion]]. The evil is not merely outside the person. It amplifies the isolation already present in the modern social world.

## Tokyo Becoming Empty

The city in *Pulse* is not a lively cyberpunk maze. It becomes sparse, gray, evacuated, and emotionally unreachable. Crowded modernity gives way to an uncanny emptiness.

This connects the film to [[Alienating City]]. Tokyo is not just the setting; it is the visible form of social thinning, where connection technologies coexist with the disappearance of real presence.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Pulse* belongs beside *[[Serial Experiments Lain]]* as a darker answer to networked life. *Lain* asks whether the network can become a false spiritual environment; *Pulse* asks whether technological connection can preserve people only as lonely ghosts.
