# The Third Wave

***The Third Wave*** is Alvin Toffler's 1980 book describing civilizational change in three broad waves: agricultural, industrial, and post-industrial/information society. It is useful to the graph because it gives a transition model for the world that cyberpunk and technocratic theory often dramatize.

## First, Second, and Third Waves

Toffler's first wave is agricultural civilization; the second is industrial civilization; the third is post-industrial, electronic, decentralized, and information-driven. The waves overlap and produce conflict.

This connects the book to [[Technetronic Society]], the concept already implied by *[[Between Two Ages]]*. Both works describe power shifting from industrial machinery toward information, communication, and knowledge systems.

## De-Massification

Toffler argues that third-wave society breaks down mass industrial forms: mass production, mass media, mass education, and centralized bureaucratic patterns.

This makes the book a useful counterpoint to older images of [[Technological Dystopia]]. Control may become less factory-like and more networked, customized, informational, and distributed.

## Prosumer and Information Society

The "prosumer" and the rise of information systems suggest a world where production and consumption, work and home, media and identity begin to blur.

This links to [[Mediated Reality]] and [[Language as Control System]]. Social life increasingly happens through codes, interfaces, messages, and information environments.

## Transition Conflict

The clash between second-wave and third-wave institutions creates dislocation, political conflict, and new elites.

This connects to [[Elite Network Historiography]] and [[Global Problematique]]. Social transformation produces both real problems and new classes of people who claim expertise in managing them.
