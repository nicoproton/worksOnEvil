# Designed Mortality

Designed Mortality names the manufactured limit placed on artificial or engineered beings: lifespan is not simply natural finitude, but a control feature built by makers. It belongs between [[Disposable Artificial Person]] and [[Artificial Immortality]] because the made being's demand for more life exposes the creator's power over death.

Use this link when a work treats lifespan, expiry, or death as part of the design of a manufactured person.

*[[Blade Runner]]* is the clearest case: replicants are made with shortened lives, and Roy Batty's demand for more life becomes the film's theological center. *[[Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep]]* shares the engineered android problem, even though the novel gives more weight to empathy, animals, religion, and extinction guilt than the film does.

The concept should not replace [[Disposable Artificial Person]]. It sharpens one part of that pattern: the maker does not only manufacture laboring bodies, but also manufactures their mortality.
