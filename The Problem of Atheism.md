# The Problem of Atheism

***The Problem of Atheism*** is Augusto Del Noce's 1964 philosophical study of atheism, modernity, rationalism, and the history of philosophy. It belongs in this graph because Del Noce treats atheism not as one private opinion among others, but as a decisive modern option that reorganizes reason, history, politics, and the meaning of man.

Del Noce is not a magisterial Catholic source, but he is a Catholic philosopher whose work helps interpret the same crisis addressed by the encyclical branch: what happens when modern reason tries to secure itself by excluding fallenness, revelation, metaphysics, and transcendence.

## Atheism as Problem, Not Destiny

The central move is already in the title. Atheism is a problem to be interpreted, not the necessary conclusion of mature thought. Del Noce resists the modern story in which philosophy, science, and historical development must naturally move from religion toward immanence.

This makes the book important for [[Progress as Secular Providence]]. Atheism often justifies itself by turning history into evidence: religion belongs to childhood, metaphysics to an obsolete age, and secular reason to maturity. Del Noce disputes that script. The supposed progress of unbelief is itself a philosophical construction.

## Rationalism and the Refusal of the Fall

Del Noce's deeper claim is theological as well as philosophical. Modern rationalism becomes anti-Christian when it rejects the fallen condition of man. If there is no wound in human nature that requires redemption, then evil can be redescribed as ignorance, bad institutions, repression, or technical backwardness.

This connects the book to [[Reason Ordered to Truth]]. Del Noce is not attacking reason. He is arguing that reason becomes false to itself when it treats Christianity's account of man as a primitive obstacle rather than as a truth with philosophical consequences.

It also touches [[False Salvation Through Systems]]. Once evil is treated mainly as ignorance, repression, or social defect, salvation becomes a project of education, therapy, revolution, planning, or technique.

## From Libertinism to Enlightenment

Del Noce is especially useful because he sees a transformation from libertine atheism into rationalized atheism. Rebellion against Christian order does not remain merely transgressive. It can become respectable, systematic, historical, and pedagogical.

That gives this book a deep link to [[Libertine Rationalism]]. The Sadean branch makes appetite philosophical; Del Noce explains how anti-Christian revolt can pass into rationalist and progressive forms that no longer look like libertinism but preserve its refusal of received moral order.

## Against Atheist Humanism

The book belongs beside *[[The Drama of Atheist Humanism]]*, though the emphasis differs. Henri de Lubac reads Feuerbach, Marx, Nietzsche, Comte, and Dostoevsky as a drama in which man tries to exalt himself by removing God. Del Noce gives a more philosophical genealogy of how atheism becomes plausible as the supposed destiny of modern reason.

The shared point is that atheism does not leave man unchanged. It reshapes anthropology. Man becomes a being to be liberated, conditioned, educated, or reconstructed within history.

## Why It Belongs Here

*The Problem of Atheism* should be used as a root diagnostic node. It helps explain why the graph repeatedly finds the same pattern in different domains: political revolution, technocratic planning, sexual liberation, scientism, and therapeutic systems often depend on an earlier decision about what man is and whether he needs redemption.

The note should not be used to claim that every modern work is atheist in the same way. Its value is sharper: it shows how a culture can make atheism appear historically necessary, then build systems of salvation on that assumption.
