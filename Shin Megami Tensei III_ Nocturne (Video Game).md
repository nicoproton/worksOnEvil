# Shin Megami Tensei III: Nocturne (Video Game)

***Shin Megami Tensei III: Nocturne*** (known as *Lucifer's Call* in Europe) is a 2003 role-playing game. It begins with the "Conception," an apocalyptic event that transforms modern Tokyo into the Vortex World, a spherical realm inhabited by demons. The protagonist, transformed into a half-demon "Demi-fiend," must navigate this chaotic new world and align with various factions, each proposing a "Reason" for the creation of a new world. The game is a profound exploration of creation, destruction, and the philosophical underpinnings of different societal ideals.

## The Vortex World After the Conception

The entire narrative revolves around the Conception and the struggle to establish a new world order based on a chosen Reason. The old world has ended, and the Vortex World becomes a suspended arena in which rival principles compete to define what reality should become.

This makes the game a strong case for [[Cosmic Law and Chaos]]. The conflict is not simply good against evil, but a contest among world-ordering principles.

The Vortex World is not a normal post-apocalypse. It is a womb-world: the old city has been inverted into a spherical interior where demons, souls, ruins, and ideologies circulate before a new creation. The game therefore turns apocalypse into gestation. Destruction is not the end of history, but the condition for choosing what kind of history can begin again.

That structure makes the protagonist's role unusually stark. The Demi-fiend is not only surviving the ruins; he is becoming a power capable of authorizing or refusing a world. The game repeatedly asks whether survival after apocalypse requires allegiance to a metaphysical principle, or whether refusal itself can become a principle.

## Reasons as False Salvation

Each "Reason" (e.g., Musubi, Yosuga, Shijima) represents a distinct philosophical system for the new world. While each promises a form of salvation or ideal society, they all come with inherent costs, limitations, and often extreme consequences for those who don't fit. These are powerful examples of [[False Salvation Through Systems]], where ideological purity can lead to new forms of oppression or emptiness, despite their proponents' conviction.

Musubi promises solitary interior worlds, protecting the self from dependence by making each person sovereign within private reality. Yosuga promises a world of strength, hierarchy, and beautiful domination. Shijima promises stillness, unity, and the quieting of individual turbulence.

Each Reason grasps a real desire: freedom from violation, order against weakness, peace against chaos. The danger is that each desire becomes total. A partial good is elevated into a world-principle, and everything that does not fit must be excluded from creation.

## Self-Imprisoning Domination

Characters who become consumed by their chosen Reason, or who seek to impose their will absolutely on the new world, often fall into a form of [[Self-Imprisoning Domination]]. Their unwavering adherence to an ideal, however noble it may seem, can blind them to alternatives and trap them in a rigid, often destructive, path, making them prisoners of their own ideology.

The sponsors of the Reasons do not merely argue for ideas; they are remade by them. Their loneliness, resentment, fear, or longing hardens into cosmology. The result is a peculiar form of imprisonment: to become powerful enough to create a world, one must first become narrow enough to desire only that world.

This is where the game differs from simpler choice systems. The alignments are not only political options; they are spiritual reductions. Each path asks what part of the human person must be sacrificed to make a perfectly coherent cosmos.

## The Great Will and Impersonal Order

The Great Will and the cosmic cycle of creation and destruction should not be forced into machine-as-god imagery. The better issue is impersonal metaphysical order: the world can be remade, but only by submitting to a principle that may reduce persons to material for cosmic design.

That impersonal order gives the game its coldness. Even rebellion against a particular Reason happens inside a larger cycle that expects worlds to be born, judged, destroyed, and replaced. The player is free in one sense, but the frame of freedom is already cosmic procedure.

For the graph, *Nocturne* is strongest as a study of false salvation at metaphysical scale. It asks not merely which regime should rule society, but which principle deserves to become reality's architecture.
