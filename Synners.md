# Synners

Pat Cadigan's *Synners* is a 1991 cyberpunk novel about media artists, corporate entertainment, hackers, and the arrival of direct brain-computer interface through neural sockets. It belongs in the graph because interface technology is not treated as a new toy. It changes the conditions of art, labor, infection, embodiment, and consciousness.

## Media Enters the Nervous System

The key technology in *Synners* lets images and performances move directly through the brain. Media no longer remains outside the person as screen, tape, or broadcast. It reaches the nervous system as both creative instrument and danger.

This makes the novel a strong case of [[Inward Life as Data]]. Creativity, perception, and expression become technically extractable and distributable. The artist's inner image can become media product.

## The Interface Has a Body

The novel matters because it does not simply celebrate disembodied cyberspace. The sockets are bodily. They alter vulnerability, labor, illness, and dependence. Information moves through flesh.

That gives *Synners* a precise relation to [[Media-Flesh Fusion]]. The interface is not a decorative cyberpunk device; it opens a path for machine and nervous system to affect one another.

## Corporate Entertainment as Capture

The corporation's interest in neural media is not neutral patronage. Direct access to mind and perception becomes the next entertainment market, the next format, the next thing to package and sell.

This connects the novel to [[Corporate Sovereignty]], though in a media-industrial form rather than a police or military form. Corporate power moves toward the interior because the interior has become technically marketable.

## Infection Between Humans and Machines

The novel's strongest depth lies in the possibility that the interface does not only transmit art. It can transmit sickness between biological and technical systems. The boundary between computer virus, brain illness, media signal, and social contagion becomes unstable.

This brings the work close to Burroughs and *[[Videodrome]]*. In all three, media does not merely persuade; it inhabits, infects, and reprograms.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Synners* belongs in the graph as one of the cyberpunk works that understands interface as a civilizational threshold. It asks what happens when image, commerce, machine, and nervous system become one circuit.
