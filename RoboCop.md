# RoboCop

*RoboCop* is Paul Verhoeven's 1987 film about Alex Murphy, a Detroit police officer murdered by criminals and rebuilt by Omni Consumer Products as a corporate-owned law-enforcement cyborg. It belongs in the graph because it makes the commercialization of life brutally literal: a dead or nearly dead man becomes product, property, weapon, and advertisement.

## The Police Body as Corporate Property

OCP does not simply build a machine. It takes Murphy's ruined body and turns him into a law-enforcement asset. His memories, face, name, pain, and moral agency are subordinated to corporate design.

This makes the film a strong case of [[Disposable Artificial Person]]. Murphy is not an android made from scratch, but his rebuilt body lets the corporation treat his remaining personhood as legally and commercially irrelevant.

## Corporate Sovereignty in Detroit

The film's Detroit is not governed only by public law. OCP wants to control police, redevelopment, security, and the future city of Delta City. The corporation acts like a private state that can absorb public functions and define urban life around profit.

This connects the film to [[Corporate Sovereignty]]. OCP's evil is not only greed; it is the replacement of civic obligation by ownership and executive strategy.

## Commercial Satire and Hollow Public Life

The fake news clips and advertisements matter because they show a society already trained to consume disaster, violence, war, sex, and politics as media fragments. The horror of Murphy's transformation appears inside a world where nearly everything can be made into content.

This gives *RoboCop* a relation to [[Mediated Reality]]. Public reality is encountered through commercial packaging, and even corporate violence can appear as product narrative.

## Flesh, Steel, and Memory

Murphy's body becomes a machine, but the film's drama depends on the return of memory, grief, and moral agency through the machine-body. Technology does not erase him completely. His personhood reappears as a claim against the system that uses him.

This places the film near [[Media-Flesh Fusion]]. The body is not merely represented by technology; it is rebuilt through it. The conflict is whether the remaining person can exceed the corporate apparatus that houses him.

## Why It Belongs Here

*RoboCop* belongs in the graph as a major corporate-cyberpunk node. It joins the alienating city, the commodified body, the privatized state, and the manufactured weapon into one sharp image: the person resurrected as product, then struggling to become a person again.
