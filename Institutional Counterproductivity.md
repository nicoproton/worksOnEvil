# Institutional Counterproductivity

Institutional Counterproductivity names the pattern in which an institution created to serve a human good begins to damage that good once it becomes too dominant, professionalized, compulsory, or self-protective. The school can weaken learning, medicine can weaken health, transportation can weaken mobility, and welfare can weaken mutual help.

This bridge belongs near [[False Salvation Through Systems]], but it is more precise. The institution may begin from a real good and may still do real good. The danger appears when the institutional form becomes so authoritative that the good itself is identified with the service, credential, procedure, or expert system.

Use this link when:

- an institution monopolizes the definition of the good it was meant to serve;
- professional service weakens ordinary competence, local judgment, family practice, or mutual aid;
- reform expands the institution while deepening the dependency it claims to solve;
- the person becomes client, patient, student, user, case, or beneficiary before he can act as a responsible agent;
- a system's success produces the conditions for a deeper failure.

Ivan Illich is the central non-fiction anchor. *[[Deschooling Society]]* gives the educational form, *[[Medical Nemesis]]* gives the medical form, *[[Tools for Conviviality]]* gives the general theory of tools and institutions, and *[[Energy and Equity]]* gives the transportation form.

The concept is close to [[Subsidiarity]], but not identical. Subsidiarity gives the Catholic social principle that higher orders should help rather than absorb lower ones. Institutional counterproductivity describes the concrete mechanism by which absorption becomes harmful.

This bridge also clarifies why the graph should not treat every institution as evil. The problem is not school, medicine, transport, expertise, or coordination as such. The problem is institutional excess: the good becomes inaccessible except through the system that now deforms it.
