# The 120 Days of Sodom

***The 120 Days of Sodom*** (*Les 120 Journées de Sodome*) is Sade's extreme unfinished work of libertine enclosure, written in the Bastille and published long after his death. Its graph value is not shock but structure: sovereign elites create a closed world where persons are reduced to classified material for appetite and domination.

## The Sealed Anti-World

The work belongs centrally to [[Closed World of Absolute License]]. The libertines withdraw into an enclosed space where law, family, religion, pity, and public witness are suspended. The enclosure is essential. Absolute license requires a world cut off from ordinary moral relation.

This makes the work more than private vice. It is a miniature anti-civilization.

## Classification and Domination

The work is also Sadean in its cold systematizing. Desire is catalogued, staged, and administered. This gives it a relation to [[Weaponized Rationalism]] through [[Libertine Rationalism]]: reason serves not truth or prudence, but the organization of appetite.

That is the point of contact with later technocratic nightmares. The content is different, but the structure is recognizable: an elite treats human beings as material after excluding every higher limit.

## Exclusion from Personhood

The victims are not merely harmed. They are placed outside the moral community of persons who count. That gives the work a dark relation to [[Purification by Exclusion]], not because it promises purity in the ordinary utopian sense, but because the libertine enclosure defines who may be used without limit.

## Why It Belongs Here

*The 120 Days of Sodom* belongs only if handled narrowly. It should not become a general node for transgression. Its specific contribution is the enclosed elite anti-world: rationalized appetite, total permission, and the transformation of persons into consumable matter.
