# The Abolition of Man

***The Abolition of Man*** is C.S. Lewis's 1943 work of moral and educational criticism. Lewis argues that modern attempts to debunk value judgments as mere subjective feeling risk producing "men without chests": people whose intellect and appetite are no longer governed by trained moral affections.

The work is important for notes on [[Weaponized Rationalism]] and [[Scientific World State]] because Lewis links the conquest of nature to the conquest of humanity. If moral law is treated as conditioning, then the people with technical power become conditioners of everyone else.

It also belongs near [[Reason Ordered to Truth]], though as an Anglican philosophical ally rather than a Catholic doctrinal source. Lewis's Tao is a defense of objective value: reason does not become more rational by dissolving moral judgment into feeling, appetite, or social conditioning.

*[[That Hideous Strength]]* adds the fictional and demonic completion of this argument. The Conditioners do not stand safely outside the process they begin. Once humanity is treated as raw material, the ruling elite can also become raw material for powers stronger than themselves. That is the novel's form of [[False Apotheosis]].

The novel also dramatizes the personal cost of abolition through [[Encounter vs Manipulation]]. Once value is dismissed, people are no longer met as persons; they become material to be educated, cured, conditioned, or redesigned.
