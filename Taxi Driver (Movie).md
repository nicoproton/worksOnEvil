# Taxi Driver (Movie)

***Taxi Driver*** is Martin Scorsese's 1976 neo-noir psychological thriller, starring Robert De Niro as Travis Bickle, a lonely and alienated Vietnam veteran working as a night-shift taxi driver in New York City. The film chronicles his descent into psychological instability, fueled by urban decay, social isolation, and a desire to "clean up" the city.

## Black Iron Prison

The sprawling, grimy, and morally corrupt urban landscape of New York City functions as a [[Black Iron Prison]] for Travis Bickle. He is trapped within its confines, both physically as he drives its streets and psychologically as its perceived filth and depravity fuel his growing psychosis and isolation.

## Evil as Psychic Contagion

The pervasive moral decay, violence, and exploitation Travis witnesses daily seem to act as an [[Evil as Psychic Contagion]], infecting his mind and warping his sense of reality. His attempts to purge the city's "scum" are a desperate, violent reaction to this perceived contamination.

## Self-Imprisoning Domination

Travis's rigid moral code, his self-imposed isolation, and his escalating vigilantism lead to a profound [[Self-Imprisoning Domination]]. His quest for purity and order ultimately isolates him further from genuine human connection and traps him in a cycle of violent fantasy and action, making him a prisoner of his own disturbed psyche.

## Mediated Reality

Travis's perception of the world is a highly [[Mediated Reality]]. His understanding of social norms, relationships, and justice is filtered through his profound loneliness, his limited interactions, and potentially his experiences in Vietnam, leading to a distorted view that fuels his violent impulses. The media he consumes (pornography, news) also contributes to his skewed worldview.

## Technological Dystopia (Precursor)

While not a futuristic setting, the dehumanizing aspects of the modern metropolis in *Taxi Driver* serve as a powerful precursor to themes found in [[Technological Dystopia]]. The anonymity, the overwhelming scale, and the cold indifference of the urban environment contribute to Travis's alienation, mirroring how advanced technological societies can strip individuals of their humanity and connection.
