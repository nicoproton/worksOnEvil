# Taxi Driver (Movie)

***Taxi Driver*** is Martin Scorsese's 1976 neo-noir psychological thriller, starring Robert De Niro as Travis Bickle, a lonely and alienated Vietnam veteran working as a night-shift taxi driver in New York City. The film chronicles his descent into psychological instability, fueled by urban decay, social isolation, and a desire to "clean up" the city.

## The City Through Travis

The sprawling, grimy, and morally corrupt urban landscape of New York City becomes Travis Bickle's inner world. He is trapped within its streets, but more importantly within the interpretation he imposes on them.

This makes the film a strong case for [[Alienating City]] and [[Mediated Reality]]. Travis does not simply see the city; he filters it through loneliness, disgust, pornography, news, insomnia, and violent fantasy.

## Contamination and Purity

The pervasive moral decay, violence, and exploitation Travis witnesses daily seem to infect his imagination. His desire to purge the city's "scum" is a desperate reaction to perceived contamination, but the reaction itself becomes violent and unstable.

This gives the film a careful relation to [[Evil as Psychic Contagion]]. The city is not simply evil by substance; Travis's perception of evil spreads into his own fantasies of cleansing.

## Vigilantism as Self-Imprisonment

Travis's rigid moral code, his self-imposed isolation, and his escalating vigilantism lead to a profound [[Self-Imprisoning Domination]]. His quest for purity and order ultimately isolates him further from genuine human connection and traps him in a cycle of violent fantasy and action, making him a prisoner of his own disturbed psyche.
