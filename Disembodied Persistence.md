# Disembodied Persistence

Disembodied Persistence names the hope or threat of surviving by loosening, abandoning, replacing, or uploading embodied life. It belongs under [[Artificial Immortality]] when continuity of self is sought through networks, shells, copies, memories, or informational presence rather than through ordinary bodily life.

This bridge should not be used for every virtual world. The key question is whether survival itself is imagined as separable from the body.

Use this link for:

- Network survival after death or bodily abandonment.
- Cyborg or artificial continuity that treats bodies as replaceable shells.
- Persistence as data, memory, signal, or distributed presence.
- Stories that ask whether survival without bodily vulnerability is still life.

*[[Serial Experiments Lain]]* begins with a dead girl claiming to survive in the Wired, then expands that claim into a whole metaphysics of networked selfhood. *[[Ghost in the Shell]]* reverses simple persistence: Project 2501 wants reproduction, variation, and mortality because endless copyability is not enough to be alive.

The concept is related to [[Networked Divinity]] when disembodied persistence becomes a claim to divinity, and to [[Embodied Relation]] when the work resists escape from bodily limitation.
