# Liberty Detached from Truth

Liberty Detached from Truth names the pattern in which freedom is understood as exemption from truth, law, moral order, nature, God, or the good proper to the person. It does not mean every appeal to liberty is false. It names the moment when liberty stops meaning the free pursuit of the good and begins to mean permission for the will to define good for itself.

This bridge belongs near [[Secular State Supremacy]] when false liberty becomes a public doctrine of state, law, education, speech, worship, or civil rights. It also belongs near [[Weaponized Rationalism]] when autonomous reason declares itself the judge of truth and uses liberty as permission to remake moral order.

Use this link when:

- liberty is treated as the right to choose without reference to truth or the good;
- freedom of speech, teaching, worship, or conscience is defended as if error and truth had equal moral rights;
- the state or majority claims authority because will has replaced truth as the source of public order;
- sin, appetite, domination, or self-creation is renamed freedom;
- coercive systems justify themselves by promising liberation from inherited moral limits.

*[[Libertas]]* is the main Catholic anchor. Leo XIII argues that true liberty is perfected by reason, law, grace, and the eternal law, while false liberty becomes license, slavery to sin, and political disorder.

*[[Veritatis Splendor]]* gives the moral-theological continuation. John Paul II argues that freedom collapses when detached from truth, especially when conscience, intention, circumstance, or consequence is treated as able to override the moral object of an act.

*[[Quanta Cura]]* gives the crisis-form: unrestricted liberty of opinion, worship, publication, and public will is condemned when it makes society indifferent to truth and divine law.

[[Libertine Rationalism]] is the moral and anti-Christian form. In Sade and related libertine works, appetite and domination are argued into the shape of philosophy, nature, and liberation.

Augusto Del Noce's *[[The Crisis of Modernity]]* gives the modern philosophical form. He distinguishes authority from mere power: when authority is dismissed as repression, liberty can become dependence on social pressure, appetite, technical systems, and powers that no longer need to call themselves authorities.

Joseph Ratzinger's *[[Truth and Tolerance]]* gives the pluralist-cultural form. Tolerance remains a real good, but becomes unstable when it depends on pretending that truth claims are merely private preferences with no public significance.

Jacques Maritain's *[[Education at the Crossroads]]* gives a personalist educational contrast. Freedom is not produced by leaving the will undefined; it grows through formation in truth, discipline, and the good of the person.

This bridge should not be used for every work that values freedom. It belongs where the text itself shows freedom being severed from truth, order, responsibility, or the good of the person.
