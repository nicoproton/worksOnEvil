# Functional City

Functional City names the modernist planning pattern in which urban life is analyzed into major functions such as dwelling, work, recreation, and circulation, then reorganized through zones, open space, traffic hierarchy, sunlight, hygiene, and expert coordination.

This bridge belongs under [[High-Modernist Planning]] and near [[Machine for Living]]. It is the urban version of the house-machine: the city becomes an apparatus whose separate parts are meant to serve a rationally ordered life.

## Use This Link When

- streets and neighborhoods are criticized because they mix too many functions;
- dwelling, work, recreation, and circulation become the main grammar of city form;
- health, light, air, open ground, traffic flow, and zoning justify large-scale redesign;
- the plan promises community or common good through separation and coordination;
- the critic's objection is that living urban relations cannot be reduced to functional categories.

## Le Corbusier and CIAM

*[[The City of Tomorrow and Its Planning]]* gives the dramatic early urban form: towers, traffic arteries, open space, business concentration, and administrative clarity.

*[[The Radiant City]]* gives the more general doctrine: dwelling, leisure, traffic, services, and green space are arranged as a repeatable model of modern urban life.

*[[The Athens Charter]]* codifies the functional city in portable form. It turns urban life into a planning grammar that can travel through schools, institutions, governments, and professional discourse.

*[[The CIAM Discourse on Urbanism]]* is the institutional-history node. It helps show that the functional city was not only one architect's fantasy, but part of a broader international modernist planning discourse.

[[Brasilia]] gives the graph a built capital where pieces of this grammar become political space: sectors, axes, superblocks, separated traffic, monumental civic form, and planned services.

## Critiques and Counter-Works

The functional city is one of the main targets of *[[The Death and Life of Great American Cities]]*. Jacobs argues that the mixture of uses, short blocks, sidewalks, old buildings, density, and informal contact is not urban disorder; it is the living order that functional separation often damages.

*[[A City is Not a Tree]]* gives the structural critique. Alexander argues that living cities overlap like semi-lattices, while planned cities often become clean hierarchies whose clarity comes from suppressing overlap.

*[[Architecture - Choice or Fate]]* gives the traditional-urbanist critique: functional zoning is not an unavoidable destiny of modern life, but a choice that can be judged against streets, squares, quarters, and mixed civic life.

## Why It Belongs Here

Functional City belongs because it lets the graph distinguish a specific planning logic from general modernism. Not every modern building is a functional city. The pattern is strongest when human life is divided into abstract functions and the city is redesigned as the coordinated environment for those functions.
