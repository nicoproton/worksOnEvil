# The Futurist Manifesto

***The Futurist Manifesto*** is F. T. Marinetti's 1909 declaration of Futurism, celebrating speed, danger, youth, violence, machinery, rupture, and contempt for museums, libraries, and inherited culture. It belongs in this graph as one of the clearest artistic statements of anti-traditional technological intoxication.

## Speed Against Memory

Marinetti's manifesto treats the past as a weight to be shattered. Museums, libraries, and old forms are not received as inheritance but attacked as tombs.

This makes the manifesto a direct counter-image to Tolkienian recovery. Where Tolkien's work preserves memory, language, lineage, and long defeat, Futurism wants acceleration strong enough to break memory's claim.

## Machine Ecstasy

The machine is not merely useful in Futurism. It becomes beautiful, erotic, violent, and spiritually charged. Speed and mechanical force promise a new human sensorium.

This places the manifesto near [[Domination Through the Machine]]. The machine is loved because it intensifies will and motion, not because it serves a creaturely order.

## Violence as Renewal

The manifesto's praise of war and destruction makes its progress openly anti-consolatory. Renewal comes by breaking, burning, accelerating, and shocking the old world.

This connects the work to [[Progress as Secular Providence]] in a violent ecstatic form. The future becomes a substitute absolute, and the past is judged as dead weight.

## Anti-Enchantment

Futurism does not enchant the world in Tolkien's sense. It does not recover sight of given reality. It intoxicates perception with force, noise, speed, and technological rupture.

That makes it useful beside [[Technological Dystopia]] as an artistic ancestor of later machine-worship. It is not a dystopian fiction, but it names the desire that many dystopias later fear.

## Why It Belongs Here

*The Futurist Manifesto* belongs because it gives the graph a pure anti-inheritance art document. Its revolution is not only political or aesthetic; it is a desire to remake perception and culture through machine violence.
