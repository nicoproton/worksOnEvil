# Manufactured Humanity

Manufactured Humanity names the motif in which human or human-like beings are produced, engineered, cloned, conditioned, or assembled for a designed purpose. The focus is not merely artificial life, but personhood made subordinate to production.

The motif appears in hatcheries, robot factories, genetic corporations, cloning regimes, and artificial-cell experiments. It often asks whether a made being can become morally visible as a person despite being designed as labor, weapon, caste member, or replacement.

This concept overlaps with [[Technological Dystopia]], [[Artificial Immortality]], and [[Golem Tradition]], but its central concern is the manufacture of persons as instruments.

Use more precise bridge concepts when possible. [[Engineered Caste]] applies when people are formed for social function before freedom can develop. [[Disposable Artificial Person]] applies when made beings are treated as tools or property and then become morally visible as persons. [[Weaponized Personhood]] applies when living or artificial beings are formed as weapons. [[Designed Successor Humanity]] applies when humanity itself is treated as material for a planned replacement or next stage.

Some works may still link here directly when they are broad anchors for the whole motif. But most work-notes should link to the specific bridge that explains what kind of manufacture is at stake.

[[Communal Reproduction]] is one of the motif's older civic ancestors: before hatcheries and androids, utopian cities imagine reproduction and family as public design problems.

*[[RoboCop]]* touches the motif through reconstruction rather than birth or cloning: a murdered officer is rebuilt as a corporate product, making [[Disposable Artificial Person]] the sharper bridge.

*[[Helter Skelter (Okazaki)]]* touches the concept from the side of cosmetic manufacture rather than artificial life. The body is not built in a factory, but it is remade as material for a designed public image.
