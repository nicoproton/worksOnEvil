# Integral Humanism

***Integral Humanism*** is Jacques Maritain's 1936 work on Christian humanism, secular humanism, pluralism, politics, and the possibility of a renewed Christian presence in modern society. It belongs in this graph because it gives a Catholic answer to one of the graph's central questions: how can concern for man avoid becoming anti-human when it rejects man's spiritual and supernatural end?

Maritain is not a magisterial source, but he is a major Catholic philosopher whose political and social thought influenced Christian democracy and later Catholic social reflection. This note should therefore be used carefully: it is a theory-bearing Catholic work, not an encyclical.

## Humanism Must Be Integral

Maritain's central claim is that secular humanism becomes partial when it excludes the spiritual dimension of man. If man is treated only as citizen, producer, economic agent, biological organism, or historical subject, then the promised liberation diminishes the person.

This gives the book a strong relation to [[Personalist Common Good]]. The person belongs to society and history, but cannot be fully defined by them.

It also connects to [[False Salvation Through Systems]]. A system that claims to save man while refusing the whole truth of man will tend to save a reduced image of him.

## New Christendom and Plural Society

The book is not a nostalgic call to restore medieval political forms. Maritain asks how Christian truth could inform public life in a plural modern society where not everyone shares the same faith.

This places the work near [[Christian Civil Order]], but with a more modern and contested problem: cooperation among people who do not share the same ultimate premises.

The connection should stay precise. *Integral Humanism* is not the same as the older papal condemnations of liberal modernity, and it should not be treated as if it solved every tension between Catholic social order and pluralism.

## Against Partial Humanisms

The book belongs beside the Catholic critique of atheist humanism. Maritain's concern overlaps with de Lubac and Del Noce: when God is removed in order to exalt man, the human person is often narrowed rather than liberated.

This links the work to [[Religion of Humanity]] by contrast. Positivist or secular-humanitarian religion can preserve devotion to man while losing the transcendent source that makes man intelligible.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Integral Humanism* belongs because the graph contains many false humanisms: scientific world state, evolutionary humanism, secular providence, atheistic communism, therapeutic management, and affluent nihilism. Maritain gives a Catholic political-philosophical response: the humanism that can truly defend man must be open to the whole person, including his spiritual end.
