# Divini Redemptoris

***Divini Redemptoris*** is Pope Pius XI's 1937 encyclical "On Atheistic Communism." It belongs in this graph because it gives a Catholic diagnosis of communism not merely as an economic error, but as a materialist false salvation that promises justice and earthly paradise while denying God, the spiritual person, family, property, natural law, and Christian civilization.

This note uses the official English text published by the Vatican.

## False Redemption After Real Misery

Pius XI begins by admitting that communism spreads partly because the modern social order has left real misery unresolved. The unequal distribution of goods, worker exploitation, and the failures of liberal economics create a field in which revolutionary promises become persuasive.

That is why the encyclical should not be read as a denial of the social question. It belongs after *[[Rerum Novarum]]* and *[[Quadragesimo Anno]]*. The Church has already said that industrial injustice is real. *Divini Redemptoris* asks what happens when the remedy becomes a materialist anti-Christian system.

This gives the note its main link to [[Atheistic Communism]].

## A False Messianic System

Pius calls communism a false messianic idea. It offers justice, equality, fraternity in labor, and a classless future. These promises carry a kind of pseudo-religious energy because they address real suffering while redirecting hope toward an earthly collective paradise.

That places the encyclical near [[False Salvation Through Systems]]. The promise is not only political efficiency. It is salvation by system: suffering will be overcome, history will reach synthesis, and humanity will be remade by revolutionary organization.

The danger is that this salvation abolishes the very goods it claims to restore. The person is not redeemed; he is absorbed into the material and collective process.

## Materialism Against the Person

The philosophical root is dialectical and historical materialism. Pius describes a world where matter alone is real, society evolves through conflict, and moral law is a product of economic conditions rather than a participation in divine or natural law.

This gives the encyclical a strong relation to [[Life Redesigned by Ideology]]. If man is only a material and social phenomenon, then family, property, education, religion, and moral restraint can all be redesigned according to the historical program.

It also gives the note a negative contrast with [[Christian Civil Order]]. In the Catholic account, society exists for the person under God. In the communist account as Pius presents it, the person becomes a part of the social-economic whole.

## Family, Education, and the Collectivity

One of the encyclical's most important graph connections is [[Administered Human Formation]]. Pius argues that atheistic communism treats marriage and family as artificial products of an economic system, withdraws woman and child into collective production and care, and denies parents a primary right over education.

The child becomes a site of ideological formation. Education belongs not first to parents under God, but to the community or state acting in the name of the collective future.

This links the encyclical to [[Secular State Supremacy]], but in a more total form. The state is not merely claiming jurisdiction over public education; it is forming the new human being for a materialist social order.

## The State That Will Wither Uses Total Power

Pius notes the contradiction in communist political theory. The state is said to be temporary and destined to wither away after the classless society is achieved. But until that future arrives, state power becomes the most effective instrument for enforcing the system.

This is a central instance of [[Liberty Detached from Truth]]. Liberation is promised, but in practice the person loses liberty, dignity, property, religion, parental authority, and even moral standing outside the collective program.

The encyclical therefore treats revolutionary freedom as a deception. The system claims to free workers from oppression, but it places them under a power that can command labor, education, speech, conscience, and social belonging.

## Propaganda and Intellectual Capture

Pius gives real attention to propaganda. Communism spreads through pamphlets, reviews, cinema, theater, radio, schools, universities, international organizations, and adaptation to local conditions. It also exploits real grievances and partial truths.

This connects carefully to [[Public Opinion Engineering]]. The encyclical is not a technical media theory, but it does identify a coordinated attempt to shape social imagination through education, art, media, intellectual prestige, and selective presentation of facts.

The point should not become a loose conspiracy node. The graph value is more precise: a total ideology needs formation, and formation works through institutions of attention and interpretation.

## Anti-Religious by Nature

For Pius XI, atheistic communism is anti-religious by nature, not merely by accident. Religion is treated as an obstacle because it points beyond the earthly paradise promised by the system. The Church, family, worship, art, memory, and clergy therefore become targets when communism gains power.

This is where the encyclical touches [[Secular State Supremacy]] most sharply. Civil power is no longer merely indifferent to religion. It becomes hostile because religion preserves a higher loyalty, a supernatural hope, and a moral law the system did not create.

The note also belongs near [[Hollowed Order]]. Communism may keep social organization, production, law, education, and public purpose, but in Pius's account those forms are emptied of God, natural law, and the spiritual dignity of the person.

## Catholic Social Doctrine as Counter-System

Pius contrasts communism with Catholic teaching on God, the spiritual soul, personal dignity, family, property, association, state authority, and social-economic justice. The Church does not offer a technical economic plan, but she gives principles by which economic and political systems can be judged.

This places the encyclical firmly under [[Economic Order Under Moral Law]]. The Catholic answer to poverty and exploitation is not terror, class hatred, abolition of property, or state absorption. It is social justice, charity, associations, just wages, rights, duties, and the common good.

Here *Divini Redemptoris* completes a triad with *Rerum Novarum* and *Quadragesimo Anno*: labor must be defended, social order must be reconstructed, but the reconstruction must not become materialist redemption by force.

## Spiritual Renewal Before Technical Remedy

The later sections turn from diagnosis to remedy. Pius insists on renewal of Christian life, detachment from earthly goods, charity, justice, Catholic social action, Catholic press and education, and the formation of workers and the poor in truth.

This is important for the graph because the remedy is not another total system. It is not a rival machine. The Church's answer begins with conversion, charity, justice, and the restoration of social life under God.

That makes *Divini Redemptoris* a strong contrast to [[False Salvation Through Systems]]. False systems try to save humanity by control. Pius argues that society must be healed by truth, grace, justice, charity, and rightly ordered institutions.

## Why It Matters Here

*Divini Redemptoris* adds the anti-communist branch to the encyclical map:

- it explains communism as materialist false salvation rather than only economic policy;
- it links class struggle, propaganda, state power, family absorption, and anti-religion into one system;
- it distinguishes real concern for workers from revolutionary reconstruction;
- it strengthens [[Economic Order Under Moral Law]] by showing what Catholic social teaching rejects on its left flank;
- it connects the social branch back to the vault's wider concern with systems that promise liberation while abolishing the person.

Its role is not to make every socialist or communal work equivalent to Bolshevism. Its role is to name the particular pattern Pius XI is attacking: an atheistic, materialist, totalizing social redemption that tries to build a new humanity by removing God.

Its 1937 companion is *[[Mit Brennender Sorge]]*, which attacks a different false absolute: not class and materialist redemption, but race, people, state, and national myth raised into [[Political Idolatry]].
