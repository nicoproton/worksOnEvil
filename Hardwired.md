# Hardwired

Walter Jon Williams's *Hardwired* is a 1986 cyberpunk novel set after Earth has been defeated by orbital corporate powers. Its world is full of smugglers, pilots, cybernetic interfaces, private war, and people surviving under a post-national order where corporations and orbital interests dominate the ground.

## Corporate War After the Nation

The novel's world is useful for the graph because political defeat has left corporate and military power fused. The old nation-state no longer provides a meaningful moral or civic frame. Power belongs to organizations that can own weapons, infrastructure, bodies, contracts, and access.

This makes *Hardwired* a strong case of [[Corporate Sovereignty]]. The corporation does not simply corrupt the state; it occupies the space where sovereignty used to be.

## Bodies Adapted to Combat and Transport

The cyberpunk body in *Hardwired* is practical, damaged, wired, and militarized. Technology enters the person through vehicles, nerves, weapons, interfaces, and survival habits. The body becomes useful by becoming compatible with systems of speed, violence, and command.

This connects the novel to [[Media-Flesh Fusion]] and [[Weaponized Personhood]]. The person is not manufactured from nothing, but the body is made operational inside a military-corporate world.

## High Technology and Low Life

*Hardwired* helps define the cyberpunk contrast between extreme technology and damaged ordinary life. Advanced systems do not produce a higher human order. They produce an uneven world where technological sophistication coexists with debt, crime, exploitation, and bodily risk.

That places the novel near [[Alienating City]], even when the setting moves beyond the city itself. Cyberpunk alienation here is atmospheric: people live inside technical, economic, and military systems that have no obligation to make them whole.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Hardwired* belongs in the graph as a bridge between *[[Neuromancer]]* and later playable cyberpunk worlds like *[[Cyberpunk 2020]]*. It gives the movement a harder military-corporate form: bodies are wired for survival under powers that have replaced political order with ownership and force.
