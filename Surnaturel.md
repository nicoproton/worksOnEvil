# Surnaturel

***Surnaturel*** is Henri de Lubac's 1946 theological study of nature, grace, the supernatural, and man's final end. It belongs in this graph because it gives a deep Catholic reason why merely immanent humanisms are insufficient: man cannot be finally understood as a closed natural being with only a natural destiny.

This note needs a caution. *Surnaturel* was controversial, associated by critics with nouvelle theologie, and de Lubac suffered restrictions in the 1950s. Later, his work was rehabilitated in significant ways and he became a major influence around Vatican II and a cardinal. For the graph, the work should be treated as an important Catholic theological intervention, not as a magisterial document.

## Nature Is Not a Closed System

De Lubac's central concern is the relation between human nature and supernatural beatitude. He argues against treating man as if he had a self-contained natural end that could be understood apart from his call to God.

This makes the work the main anchor for [[Supernatural End of Man]]. Human nature is real, created, and good, but man is not fulfilled by nature alone.

## Grace Is Gift, Not Possession

The book does not make grace a human possession or natural right. The supernatural remains gift. The tension is precisely that man is ordered beyond himself and yet cannot claim God as something owed by nature.

That distinction matters for the graph. It prevents [[Supernatural End of Man]] from becoming another technique of fulfillment. Grace is not a higher technology, hidden capacity, or evolutionary upgrade.

## The Danger of Pure Immanence

The graph connection is strongest by contrast. If man is imagined as self-contained, then politics, science, therapy, art, revolution, or economic comfort can begin to look like enough.

This links *Surnaturel* to [[False Salvation Through Systems]] and to *[[The Drama of Atheist Humanism]]*. Atheist humanism diminishes man because it tries to exalt a creature whose supernatural end has first been denied.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Surnaturel* gives the Catholic branch a metaphysical depth that the graph needs. It explains why many secular projects fail even when they sincerely aim at human flourishing: they seek to complete man inside an immanent frame, while man is made for a fulfillment that exceeds every system.
