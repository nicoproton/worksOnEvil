# Feed

M. T. Anderson's *Feed* is a 2002 dystopian novel about teenagers in a future where most people have an internet-like corporate media system implanted directly into their brains. Its value for the graph is that the feed is not only a communication device. It shapes language, attention, desire, status, education, consumption, and the ability to imagine resistance.

## Advertising Inside Thought

The feed brings commerce into the stream of consciousness. Desire is not merely influenced by advertising from outside; it is tracked, prompted, interrupted, and formed inside the mind.

This makes the novel a strong case of [[Inward Life as Data]]. Thought and preference become accessible to corporate systems, and the person becomes legible as a consumer before becoming legible as a soul, citizen, or neighbor.

## Language Degraded by the Feed

The novel's slang and broken expressive habits matter because they show that the technology has changed formation. The characters do not simply use a network; they grow up inside a linguistic and commercial environment that narrows their ability to name reality.

This connects the novel to [[Language as Control System]]. The feed does not issue one command, but it reshapes the field of available words, desires, jokes, and assumptions.

## Corporate Formation of the Young

The feed is also educational in the darker sense: it forms young people toward consumption, distraction, and trend responsiveness. Adolescence becomes a market surface.

That places the novel near [[Administered Human Formation]] and [[Corporate Sovereignty]]. The corporation does not merely sell to pre-existing persons; it helps form what kind of persons will exist.

## Love and Resistance Under Mediation

The relationship between Titus and Violet matters because Violet is not simply a rebel with better opinions. She is trying to preserve memory, attention, language, and judgment against a system that has made those things socially costly.

This gives the novel a minor but real relation to [[Fragile Counter-Community]]. The resistance is fragile because the surrounding system has already formed most people not to understand what is being lost.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Feed* belongs in the graph because it treats the brain-interface as a change in human formation. It is consumer dystopia at the level of thought, language, desire, and adolescence.
