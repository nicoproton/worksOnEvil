# Political Justice

***An Enquiry Concerning Political Justice*** is William Godwin's 1793 political-philosophical work arguing that government, inherited institutions, punishment, property conventions, and social prejudice obstruct the free exercise of reason and moral judgment. It belongs in this graph because it gives an early radical form of perfectibility before that dream becomes poetic in Percy Shelley or cautionary in Mary Shelley.

## Reason Against Institution

Godwin's central wager is that human beings can become better as truth becomes clearer and social constraints lose their coercive power. Government appears less as a necessary guardian of order than as a machine that preserves ignorance, dependence, punishment, and manipulation.

This gives the work a strong relation to [[Progress as Secular Providence]]. History is imagined as morally improvable through the spread of reason, the weakening of inherited authority, and the liberation of private judgment.

## Perfectibility Without Incarnation

The early form of Godwin's argument pushes rational impartiality so far that ordinary affections can appear morally suspect. Family, gratitude, habit, and private attachment risk becoming local prejudices before the universal judgment of reason.

That makes the book useful beside *[[The Abolition of Man]]* by contrast. Lewis fears that moral abstraction detached from real creaturely goods prepares the way for conditioning. Godwin is not a technocrat, but his early rationalism shows how the person can be thinned when reason is imagined apart from affection, body, family, and historical belonging.

## Utopia Without Central Management

Godwin is not a planner in the later technocratic sense. His future society tends toward decentralized independence rather than bureaucratic administration. This distinction matters for the graph: his perfectibility is anti-institutional, not statist.

Even so, the work stands near [[False Salvation Through Systems]] as a precursor and question. If inherited limits are treated mainly as errors to be dissolved, liberation can begin to resemble salvation by rational reconstruction.

## The Immortality Question

The first edition's speculation about indefinite prolongation of life gives the work a strange relation to [[Death Refused as Gift]]. Godwin's immortality is not technological in the modern sense, but it follows from the same confidence that mind, reason, and perfectibility might eventually overcome bodily limitation.

This point later becomes narratively darker in *[[St. Leon]]*, where alchemical immortality and endless wealth isolate the protagonist from domestic affection.

## Shelley and the Radical Inheritance

Godwin's thought helps explain the intellectual atmosphere behind *[[Queen Mab]]*. Shelley radicalizes the hope that monarchy, priestcraft, custom, and commerce may disappear before truth and moral progress.

The connection is historical and conceptual, not identical. Godwin's prose theory supplies a rationalist vocabulary; Shelley turns that vocabulary into visionary rebellion.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Political Justice* belongs because it shows that the graph's anti-incarnational danger does not begin with machines. It can begin with a purified reason that wants justice, truth, and liberation, but has trouble receiving the embodied affections through which human beings actually live.
