# Life Redesigned by Ideology

Life Redesigned by Ideology names the pattern in which a political, philosophical, scientific, or spiritual doctrine treats ordinary life as material to be remade: work, family, housing, art, education, city form, perception, health, production, and social belonging.

This bridge sits above [[Art Into Life]], [[Machine for Living]], [[High-Modernist Planning]], [[Administered Human Formation]], and [[Scientific World State]]. It is useful when the issue is not only that art enters architecture, but that a larger worldview tells art and architecture what kind of person and society they should produce.

## More Than Architecture

Modernist architecture did not arise only from aesthetic taste. It was fed by industrialism, utopian socialism, scientific management, production ideology, secular religion, revolutionary myth, and faith in expert planning.

The shared claim is that inherited life is not simply received and repaired. It is diagnosed as obsolete, irrational, wasteful, oppressive, unhealthy, or historically exhausted. Once that diagnosis is accepted, a new class of experts, artists, engineers, planners, managers, or revolutionaries can claim authority to design the replacement.

## Different Regimes, Shared Grammar

This bridge should not claim that liberal America, Soviet Russia, Fascist Italy, Weimar modernism, and French technocratic planning were identical. They were enemies in many ways.

The deeper similarity is a grammar of reconstruction. The old world is judged by the new age. Production, hygiene, efficiency, consciousness, collective life, and historical necessity become warrants for redesign. That grammar can be translated into socialist planning, corporate efficiency, fascist spectacle, liberal institutional modernism, or technocratic administration.

## Source Currents

Saint-Simon's *[[The Industrial System]]* and *[[The New Christianity]]* give an early industrial-religious version: society should be reorganized around productive labor, science, industry, and the moral improvement of the poorest.

Fourier's *[[The New Industrial and Societary World]]* gives the social-architectural utopian version: passions, labor, domestic life, and settlement are to be reorganized through a designed community.

Taylor's *[[The Principles of Scientific Management]]* and Ford's *[[Today and Tomorrow]]* give the industrial-method version: work can be analyzed, standardized, timed, coordinated, and made into a system.

Ozenfant and Le Corbusier's *[[Apres le Cubisme]]* and the journal *[[L'Esprit Nouveau]]* give the Purist version: art, order, machine clarity, standard objects, and architecture belong to one modern discipline of form.

Arvatov's *[[Art and Production]]* and Tarabukin's *[[From the Easel to the Machine]]* give the Soviet Productivist version: art should leave the isolated easel and enter production, object-making, labor, and collective life.

Sorel's *[[Reflections on Violence]]* gives the mythic-political version: modern politics is not only rational program, but mobilizing myth, action, and rupture.

## Why It Belongs Here

Life Redesigned by Ideology belongs because it gives the graph a deeper layer beneath modernist architecture. It explains why so many different domains began to converge: art, theater, industry, housing, planning, education, media, and politics were all drawn into the belief that the new age required a newly designed human environment.
