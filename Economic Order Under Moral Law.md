# Economic Order Under Moral Law

Economic Order Under Moral Law names the Catholic social pattern in which labor, property, wages, association, poverty, and state action are judged by natural law, human dignity, family life, justice, charity, and the common good. Economic life is not morally autonomous, but neither is it merely raw material for state redesign.

This bridge belongs under [[Christian Civil Order]] when the question moves from the constitution of the state to the moral structure of work, ownership, labor, and social remedy.

Use this link when:

- labor is treated as personal and necessary rather than as a commodity only;
- private property is defended as natural while its use is ordered toward others;
- the family is treated as prior to the state and not absorbable by civil power;
- wages, rest, working conditions, and worker associations are judged by justice rather than contract alone;
- socialism or technocratic reconstruction is rejected without excusing exploitation by employers or markets.

*[[Rerum Novarum]]* is the central encyclical anchor. Leo XIII rejects socialism's abolition of private property, but also rejects the reduction of workers to instruments of gain. The remedy is religious, moral, legal, associational, and social at once.

*[[Quadragesimo Anno]]* develops the social-order form. Pius XI adds [[Subsidiarity]], a sharper critique of economic concentration, and a double rejection of both socialist absorption and economic individualism.

*[[Divini Redemptoris]]* gives the anti-communist continuation. Pius XI argues that real economic injustice must not be answered by [[Atheistic Communism]], where materialist class redemption absorbs the person, family, property, and religion into the collective program.

This concept is a counterweight to [[Life Redesigned by Ideology]]. The Catholic answer to industrial misery is not to redesign ordinary life from above according to a total theory, but to restore the moral order inside work, property, family, law, charity, and association.

It also borders [[Administered Human Formation]] where education or labor policy becomes a state instrument. The distinction is important: Catholic social order allows public authority to protect the weak, but refuses to let the state absorb the family, the Church, or smaller associations.

This bridge should not be used for every work about poverty or labor. It belongs where economic questions are explicitly ordered by moral anthropology, natural law, family, and the common good.
