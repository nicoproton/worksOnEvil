# Apres le Cubisme

***Apres le Cubisme*** is the 1918 Purist manifesto by Amedee Ozenfant and Charles-Edouard Jeanneret, later known as Le Corbusier. It belongs in this graph because it gives Le Corbusier's architectural modernism an art-theoretical prehistory.

The text argues for order, clarity, construction, stable forms, standard objects, and a disciplined alternative to late Cubist fragmentation. Its importance is that architecture, painting, machine-age objects, and modern order begin to belong to one intellectual field.

## Purism After Cubism

Purism does not reject modern art in order to return to academic realism. It tries to purify modern art by restoring order, legibility, proportion, and disciplined relation.

This makes the work a central case of [[Art Into Life]]. Painting is not isolated from modern objects and architecture. It becomes a training ground for a broader order of form.

## Standard Objects and Machine Clarity

The Purist attraction to standard objects matters for the architecture branch. Bottles, glasses, pipes, machines, and manufactured forms appear as evidence that modern life has its own grammar of clarity and type.

This places the work beside [[Machine for Living]]. Before the house becomes a machine for living, art has already learned to admire the object's purified, repeatable, industrial form.

## Le Corbusier Before the City

This is one of the best nodes for connecting Le Corbusier to wider modernist art. *[[Toward an Architecture]]* sounds architectural, but *Apres le Cubisme* shows the earlier Purist logic: order is aesthetic, moral, technical, and civilizational at once.

The journal *[[L'Esprit Nouveau]]* continues this bridge by bringing painting, architecture, design, urbanism, and modern culture into one forum.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Apres le Cubisme* belongs because it shows that Le Corbusier's architecture is not only engineering admiration or housing reform. It also grows from an art theory of purification, order, standard form, and the belief that modern life needs a new visual discipline.
