# Common Sense

***Common Sense*** is Thomas Paine's 1776 pamphlet arguing for American independence and attacking monarchy, hereditary succession, and the legitimacy of inherited political rule. It belongs in this graph because it gives the popular revolutionary form of a major modern claim: inherited authority is not sacred order but political absurdity.

## Against Hereditary Rule

Paine's attack on monarchy is also an attack on the idea that public authority can be transmitted by birth. Political inheritance appears not as stewardship, continuity, or received obligation, but as irrational privilege.

This connects the pamphlet to [[Hollowed Order]]. Paine treats hereditary monarchy as a form whose justifying good has drained away: kingship remains, but its claim to serve political order has become corruption, accident, and violence.

## Inheritance and Tolkien's Contrast

The inheritance question makes *Common Sense* a useful contrast to Tolkien. In *[[The Lord of the Rings]]* and *[[The Silmarillion]]*, lineage can carry memory, duty, stewardship, and burden; it is not automatically a right to dominate.

Paine is attacking hereditary political power, not family as such. Still, the contrast matters. Tolkien's world asks how inherited goods can be received and healed; Paine asks why inherited rule should be believed at all.

## Revolution as Public Reason

The pamphlet turns revolution into public argument. It does not rely on esoteric initiation, noble lineage, or hidden wisdom. Its force is plain language directed toward ordinary political judgment.

This places the work near [[Progress as Secular Providence]]. The old order is judged from the standpoint of future liberty, equality, and republican self-government.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Common Sense* belongs because it gives the graph the popular democratic attack on inherited order. It is not technocratic, occult, or utopian in the later sense, but it helps explain the modern suspicion that tradition and lineage conceal arbitrary power.
