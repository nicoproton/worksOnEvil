# Future Shock

***Future Shock*** is Alvin Toffler's 1970 book about the psychological and social stress produced by accelerating technological and cultural change. It is useful to the graph because it names a form of modern entropy: not simple collapse, but overload, disorientation, and the loss of stable patterns of meaning.

## Too Much Change Too Fast

Toffler's central claim is that rapid change can overwhelm human adaptation. People experience not only new tools, but new family patterns, jobs, identities, environments, and decision burdens.

This connects the book to [[Entropy and the Binding Force]]. The binding force weakens when institutions and habits change faster than people can integrate them.

## Transience

Toffler emphasizes transience: disposable goods, temporary relationships, short-lived institutions, and rapid turnover in social forms.

Dystopia can arrive not only through tyranny, but through acceleration that dissolves durable bonds.

## Information and Choice Overload

The future shocks people partly through excessive information and decision pressure. More options can produce paralysis rather than freedom.

This links to [[Mediated Reality]] and [[Simulated Reality]] in their softer social forms: reality becomes harder to inhabit because it is constantly reframed by information flows.

## Adaptation as Governance Problem

Toffler's analysis invites social planning: if acceleration harms people, institutions may need to manage transition. That solution is ambiguous.

The diagnosis of overload is real, but the system built to manage adaptation can become another apparatus of control.
