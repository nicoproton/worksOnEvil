# The Splendor of the Church

***The Splendor of the Church*** is Henri de Lubac's 1953 ecclesiological work on the Church as mystery, mother, body, and sacramentally ordered communion. It belongs in this graph because it deepens the Catholic answer to both private spirituality and secular collective systems.

## The Church Is More Than Institution

De Lubac does not deny visible Church, authority, doctrine, discipline, or hierarchy. His point is that these cannot be understood properly if the Church is reduced to a sociological institution or religious service organization.

This makes the work a strong case for [[Ecclesial Communion]]. The Church is visible, but her visibility is the visibility of a mystery, not only an administrative structure.

## Communion Without Collectivism

The book also helps clarify a major graph distinction. Catholic communion is not collectivist absorption. The Church gathers persons into Christ without turning them into interchangeable parts of a political machine.

This connects the work to [[Personalist Common Good]]. The person is fulfilled through communion, but not possessed by an immanent collective.

## A Counterpoint to Hollowed Order

The note also belongs near [[Hollowed Order]]. When ecclesial form loses sacramental and doctrinal depth, the Church can be misread as culture, power, sentiment, heritage, or bureaucracy.

De Lubac's answer is not to make the Church invisible. It is to recover the mystery carried by the visible form.

## Why It Belongs Here

*The Splendor of the Church* strengthens the Catholic branch by showing that the answer to modern reduction is not only better politics or better philosophy. It is a richer account of the Church herself as communion, body, mother, and sacramental mystery.
