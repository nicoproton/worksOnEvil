# Love in the Ruins

***Love in the Ruins: The Adventures of a Bad Catholic at a Time Near the End of the World*** is Walker Percy's 1971 comic-apocalyptic novel about Dr. Tom More, a lapsed Catholic psychiatrist in a fractured near-future America who invents the Ontological Lapsometer, a device meant to diagnose and treat spiritual and psychological disorder.

The novel belongs in this graph because it turns the modern crisis of personhood into medical satire. Society is breaking apart, but the proposed instrument of diagnosis risks becoming another way of treating spiritual disorder as an object for technique.

## A Bad Catholic at the End of the World

Tom More is not a clean saintly counterpoint to modern disorder. He is alcoholic, lustful, professionally compromised, and spiritually confused. That weakness matters. Percy does not oppose technocratic disorder with a fantasy of pure religious competence.

This gives the novel a grounded relation to [[Conscience Detached from Truth]]. Tom knows enough to recognize disorder, but he also repeatedly evades truth in his own life. The danger is both social and inward.

## The Ontological Lapsometer

The Ontological Lapsometer is the novel's brilliant graph object: a machine that claims to measure the state of the soul-like self. It is comic because it treats ontology as diagnosable data, but dangerous because such a device can become therapeutic control.

This makes the novel a strong case of [[False Cure]]. The problem it tries to address is real: persons and communities are disordered. But a cure that reduces disorder to measurable, adjustable malfunction risks bypassing repentance, relation, sacrament, and truth.

The device also belongs to [[Semiotic Dislocation]]. Tom's machine can detect signs of malaise, but the deeper question is whether signs and measurements can truly place the person.

## Fragmented America

The novel's America is split by politics, race, sexuality, religion, consumer life, and ideological caricature. Percy plays much of this as comedy, but the fragmentation is serious: people inhabit roles and camps without a shared horizon of truth.

This gives the book a relation to [[Affluent Nihilism]]. The world is not ruined because everyone is poor or enslaved. It is ruined because comfort, ideology, therapy, and appetite coexist with spiritual incoherence.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Love in the Ruins* is valuable because it joins the Catholic branch to the technocratic and therapeutic branches without becoming a simple dystopia. The instrument of cure is not merely evil; it is a comic sign of a deeper confusion. Modern man wants a diagnostic device for the soul when what he needs is conversion, truth, and rightly ordered love.
