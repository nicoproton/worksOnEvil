# Tower of Babel Archetype

The Tower of Babel archetype comes from Genesis 11, where human beings build a city and tower "with its top in the heavens" and are scattered through the confusion of language. In later literature, Babel often becomes an image of collective hubris, centralized power, technological ambition, and the collapse of shared speech.

The archetype is useful when a work imagines a project that tries to unify humanity from above while actually severing people from a deeper moral or divine order. Its signs include vertical architecture, universal language, administrative coordination, imperial building, and punishment through confusion or social breakdown.

Do not use this concept for every important tower. The key feature is not towerness by itself, but usurping ascent: an antichrist or imperial attempt to seize divine order and impose unity from below. When the tower or center is legitimate and evil tries to destroy it, use [[Cosmic Axis Under Siege]] instead.

*[[Snow Crash]]* gives the archetype a cyberpunk inversion: linguistic fragmentation becomes a defense against older forms of mass programming, while code and language converge as tools of control.
