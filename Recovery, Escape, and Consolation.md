# Recovery, Escape, and Consolation

Recovery, Escape, and Consolation are Tolkien's account of what fairy-stories give to readers. Recovery renews perception of the ordinary world; Escape defends the desire to flee prison rather than desert duty; Consolation names the joy of the happy ending, especially in the form of [[Eucatastrophe]].

## Recovery

Recovery is the restoration of clear sight. Fantasy can make familiar things strange again: trees, bread, stone, water, friendship, mortality, and home.

This connects to [[Entropy and the Binding Force]] because decay is not only physical. Perception also decays when goods become invisible through habit or possession.

## Escape

Tolkien defends escape when it is the escape of the prisoner, not the flight of the deserter. The desire to escape ugliness, mechanization, tyranny, and death can be morally serious.

Tolkien's defense of escape is partly a defense against the claim that the current industrial world is the only serious reality.

## Consolation

Consolation is not denial of grief. It is the possibility that grief is not final. In Tolkien's theory, the highest form of consolation is eucatastrophe.

This gives the graph a way to name positive resistance that is not system-building: hope, restored sight, and longing for a reality healed beyond domination.
