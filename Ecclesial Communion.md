# Ecclesial Communion

Ecclesial Communion names the Catholic pattern in which salvation, worship, doctrine, and personhood are not private possessions. The person is drawn into Christ, the Church, the Eucharist, and the communion of saints.

This bridge belongs near [[Personalist Common Good]], [[Embodied Relation]], and [[Beauty Returned to Worship]]. It is not the same as political collectivism. Communion does not absorb the person into a state, class, or system; it gathers persons into a supernatural body whose center is Christ.

Use this link when:

- Christian salvation is shown as communal rather than individualist;
- Eucharist, Church, liturgy, doctrine, and worship form persons together;
- the Church is treated as mystery and body, not merely institution, bureaucracy, or voluntary association;
- the text opposes both isolated spirituality and secular collective absorption.

Henri de Lubac's *[[Catholicism (de Lubac)]]* gives the broad doctrinal form: dogma has social dimensions because salvation itself is ecclesial and communal.

*[[Corpus Mysticum]]* gives the Eucharistic-historical form. The Eucharist and the Church must be understood together rather than separated into private devotion on one side and external institution on the other.

*[[The Splendor of the Church]]* gives the ecclesiological form. The Church is not merely a sociological organization that administers religious services; she is mystery, mother, body, and sacramental communion.

Guardini's *[[The Spirit of the Liturgy (Guardini)]]* and Ratzinger's *[[The Spirit of the Liturgy]]* give liturgical neighbors: worship forms a people through received common action rather than private religious self-expression.

Yves Congar's *[[True and False Reform in the Church]]* gives the reform-neighbor. Communion does not mean immobility, but reform becomes false when it breaks the very communion it claims to renew.

Congar's *[[Lay People in the Church]]* gives the lay-vocation neighbor. Ecclesial communion includes lay responsibility in the world, not only clerical administration or private devotion.

This concept should not be used whenever a group appears. It belongs where communion is theological and sacramental, not merely social togetherness.
