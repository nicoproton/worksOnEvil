# Religion of Humanity

Religion of Humanity names the pattern in which humanity itself becomes the object of reverence, moral devotion, ritual memory, and social service. It is not merely atheism or secular politics. It is the replacement of transcendent worship with an immanent sacred object: Humanity, progress, society, or the collective human future.

This bridge is useful when a work does not abolish religion so much as rebuilds it around mankind. Priests, catechisms, calendars, saints, rituals, moral discipline, and education may remain, but their theological center has shifted.

Use this link for:

- Systems where Humanity replaces God as the object of worship or final loyalty.
- Secular religions that preserve liturgy, priesthood, moral formation, or catechism.
- Humanitarian or positivist orders where social service becomes worship.
- Works where Catholic or Christian form remains after supernatural substance is removed.

*[[Catechism of Positive Religion]]* is the central theoretical anchor. Comte explicitly organizes positivism as a religion of Humanity, adapting Catholic structure while rejecting Catholic theology.

Henri de Lubac's *[[The Drama of Atheist Humanism]]* gives the Catholic interpretation of why this matters. Comte is not simply an eccentric system-builder; his religion of Humanity is one form of the modern attempt to dethrone God and replace Him with man.

De Lubac's theology points toward [[Supernatural End of Man]] as the deeper failure. Humanity becomes too small an object of worship because man is made for God, not for enclosure inside the collective human future.

Christopher Dawson's *[[The Gods of Revolution]]* gives a broader historical frame for the same replacement pattern. Humanity, reason, history, nation, or class can become new sacred objects when Christian culture weakens.

Jacques Maritain's *[[Integral Humanism]]* is a Catholic counterpoint. It accepts the need for a humanism adequate to modern society, but argues that humanism becomes partial and finally anti-human when it closes man inside immanence.

Saint-Simon's *[[The New Christianity]]* is an earlier neighbor rather than the same thing. It keeps more Christian vocabulary, but it already redirects religious energy toward organized social improvement and the condition of the poorest.

*[[Lord of the World]]* is the strongest fictional-theological neighbor. Benson imagines a future where humanitarian unity and worship of man prepare an Antichristic order. The link should be careful: Comte is not Benson's fictional Antichrist, but Comte helps explain why secular modernity may become religious rather than merely irreligious.

[[Political Idolatry]] is a neighboring but different form of immanent sacralization. Religion of Humanity sacralizes humanity as a universal collective; political idolatry sacralizes a particular race, people, state, nation, class, or regime.

This concept often overlaps with [[Hollowed Order]] when religious form remains after worship of God has been replaced, and with [[Scientific World State]] when social order is guided by expert or scientific administration.
