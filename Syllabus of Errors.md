# Syllabus of Errors

***Syllabus of Errors*** is the 1864 catalog of eighty condemned propositions issued with Pius IX's *[[Quanta Cura]]*. It belongs in this graph because it maps a whole field of nineteenth-century errors rather than one isolated doctrine: rationalism, naturalism, religious indifferentism, civil supremacy over the Church, secular education, civil marriage, socialism, and modern liberalism.

This note uses the official Latin text published by the Vatican as part of the *Quanta Cura* page. The document is a syllabus, not an encyclical. It gathers propositions already treated in Pius IX's allocutions, encyclicals, and apostolic letters.

## A Map of Errors Rather Than an Essay

The *Syllabus* is not written like *Aeterni Patris*, *Providentissimus Deus*, or *Humani Generis*. It does not patiently develop one argument. It lists propositions under headings and cites prior papal documents where those issues were treated.

That structure matters. The document functions almost like a negative map of modernity as Pius IX saw it: what must be rejected if Catholic doctrine, Church authority, family, education, marriage, and public order are to remain intelligible.

In graph terms, it is useful less as a prose argument and more as a hub for several bridge concepts.

## Rationalism Before the State

The first sections condemn pantheism, naturalism, absolute rationalism, and moderate rationalism. The pattern is familiar: God is collapsed into the world, divine action is denied, human reason becomes the sole arbiter of truth and good, and revelation is made imperfect or subject to indefinite progress.

That gives the *Syllabus* a real but careful connection to [[Weaponized Rationalism]]. It is not describing machines, bureaucracy, or technocracy. It is naming the philosophical root in which reason is made autonomous from God and then used to judge revelation.

It also looks forward to [[Theological Modernism]] and [[Dogmatic Relativism]]. The proposition that revelation is subject to indefinite progress already anticipates later Catholic worries about dogma being governed by historical development rather than by revealed truth.

## Religion as Private Choice

The section on indifferentism and latitudinarianism rejects the idea that each person may simply embrace whatever religion he judges true by the light of reason, or that any religion may equally lead to salvation.

In the graph, this should not be reduced to mere intolerance. The deeper issue is religious truth becoming a private option. Once religion is treated chiefly as an individual choice among equivalent forms, society no longer needs to be ordered toward the true worship of God.

That places the *Syllabus* near [[Hollowed Order]]. Religion can remain culturally present while its public claim to truth is weakened.

## Civil Society Over the Church

The largest graph contribution is [[Secular State Supremacy]]. The *Syllabus* rejects propositions in which the state defines the Church's rights, makes civil law prevail in conflicts with ecclesiastical law, controls schools, interferes with Church communication, treats marriage as a civil matter, or claims that Church and state must be separated.

This is where the document most directly speaks to the vault's broader concern with administered life. If education, family, marriage, religion, and moral law are all placed under civil competence, the state becomes more than a guardian of public peace. It becomes the final designer of the social order.

That connection should still be bounded. The *Syllabus* is not the same thing as twentieth-century planning ideology. It gives a Catholic nineteenth-century critique of the public order that makes later administered forms easier to imagine.

## Education and the Child

Several propositions concern schools and youth formation. The condemned pattern is that public education should be placed fully under civil authority and separated from the Catholic faith and the Church's power.

This gives the note a precise link to [[Administered Human Formation]]. The child becomes a site where the state and the Church compete over the formation of judgment, loyalty, conscience, and the meaning of human life.

The connection is one of principle rather than genre. The *Syllabus* is not a dystopian story. It identifies the political-theological condition under which education becomes a civil instrument detached from supernatural truth.

## Modern Liberalism as Final Reconciliation

The final propositions reject the claim that the Roman Pontiff can and ought to reconcile himself with progress, liberalism, and modern civilization, if those terms mean secular emancipation from Catholic truth and authority.

This gives the *Syllabus* a tense relation to [[Progress as Secular Providence]]. The document is not rejecting every improvement, discovery, or reform. It rejects progress when progress means that the Church must accept the modern age as judge over revelation, morals, and public order.

That distinction will matter later when this branch reaches *Libertas* and Catholic social teaching. The Catholic counter-line is not simply anti-change. It rejects the enthronement of historical modernity as a source of truth.

## Why It Matters Here

The *Syllabus of Errors* gives the graph a nineteenth-century Catholic index of the modern problem:

- reason without God;
- revelation under progress;
- religion as private option;
- the state as final public authority;
- education detached from the Church;
- marriage and family absorbed into civil law;
- progress treated as a demand that Catholicism reconcile itself to the age.

Its best use in the vault is as a negative map. It shows how many themes that appear separately in later nodes already belonged together for Pius IX: rationalism, secular politics, public education, moral law, family, and modern liberal civilization.
