# Europe a Prophecy

***Europe a Prophecy*** is William Blake's 1794 illuminated poem on repression, war, revolution, and apocalyptic upheaval in Europe. It belongs in this graph because it darkens the revolutionary hope of *[[America a Prophecy]]*: the old order is diseased, but its breaking is terrifying as well as liberating.

## Repression Before Eruption

Blake presents Europe as a world of bound energies: religious restraint, political oppression, sexual repression, and imaginative confinement. Revolution appears because life has been chained.

This links the poem to [[Hollowed Order]]. The forms of religion, law, and society remain, but they have become restrictive shells rather than living orders.

## Sexual Repression and Political Bondage

The poem is especially useful for the pattern you noticed: sexual freedom, magic, and revolution are not separate topics in Blake. Sexual repression belongs to the same order that suppresses prophecy, imagination, and political life.

This should not yet become a new bridge by itself. For now, it belongs near [[Disordered Love]] in argument and near [[Weaponized Rationalism]] when restraint becomes abstract law severed from embodied life. Blake's claim is not simply "license is good"; it is that dead law misnames living desire as evil.

## Apocalypse of the Old Order

The revolutionary energy in *Europe* is not clean optimism. It appears amid war, fear, and convulsion. The old order is spiritually sick, but its dissolution does not automatically produce peace.

That places the poem near [[World Wound and Long Decline]]. Blake sees a wounded civilization whose political crises are signs of deeper spiritual disorder.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Europe a Prophecy* belongs because it prevents Blake's revolution from looking simple. The poem keeps together liberation and catastrophe: the breaking of repression may be necessary, but the world that emerges from the break remains unstable and wounded.
