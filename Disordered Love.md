# Disordered Love

Disordered Love names evil as love turned toward the wrong object, wrong measure, or wrong mode. The desire may be for self, power, safety, knowledge, beauty, belonging, or freedom, but it becomes destructive when loved above the good that should order it.

This bridge belongs under [[Privatio Boni]] and is especially close to Augustine's account of evil. The point is not that desire is evil, but that a real good becomes ruinous when loved in isolation from truth, humility, charity, or dependence.

Use this link when evil appears as refusal to receive love, wounded love becoming cruelty, possessive self-love, pride, or a desire that collapses into domination.

In *[[Lilith (MacDonald)]]*, Lilith's beauty and sovereignty become deathly because she refuses humility and reception. In *[[The Sorrows of Young Werther]]*, feeling and impossible love become absolute measures of existence. In *[[Madame Bovary]]*, Emma's desire for beauty, love, and intensity becomes destructive because it is governed by romantic self-fiction rather than by truth or charity. In *[[La Regenta]]*, Ana's desire for love and spiritual significance becomes entangled with seduction, vanity, and corrupt guidance. In *[[Les Fleurs du mal]]*, desire repeatedly turns toward beauty, sin, intoxication, blasphemy, and death as possible exits from spiritual suffocation. In *[[A Season in Hell]]*, the desire for freedom and visionary transformation turns into an inward inferno. In *[[The Picture of Dorian Gray]]*, beauty and youth are loved as absolutes until they become hollow. In *[[Apollo's Song]]*, Shogo's cruelty grows from the absence and rejection of love. In *[[EarthBound Beginnings]]*, Giegue's evil is bound to a severed maternal bond. In *[[Mother 3]]*, Porky's pursuit of domination and safety ends in unreachable isolation.

This concept is not the same as [[Self-Imprisoning Domination]], though they often overlap. Disordered Love names the inward misordering; Self-Imprisoning Domination names the trap produced by seeking power through that misordering.
