# The Shockwave Rider

***The Shockwave Rider*** is John Brunner's 1975 novel about data networks, identity, social instability, predictive systems, and information-age control. It belongs in this graph as a transition from industrial dystopia to technetronic society.

## Before Cyberpunk, After Bureaucracy

The novel appears before the classic cyberpunk wave, but it already imagines a society in which computers, databases, surveillance, forecasting, and social mobility reshape personal identity. It narrows and intensifies the information-age anxiety Brunner had already built on a larger social scale in *[[Stand on Zanzibar]]*. Its world is not simply ruled by machines; it is ruled through information.

The protagonist, Nick Haflinger, is valuable because he understands the networked order from inside. He can move through aliases, records, and systems, which makes him both a product of the system and a threat to it.

## Society as Information Field

The novel imagines a world where data, networks, forecasts, records, and institutional classifications shape social existence.

This gives the book a different flavor from older industrial dystopias. The factory is no longer the main image of control. The file, the prediction, the identity record, and the network become more important.

This makes it a strong fiction counterpart to [[Technetronic Society]]. Power is no longer only factory, army, or laboratory; it moves through information systems.

## Identity Under Network Pressure

Identity in the novel is unstable because records, aliases, systems, and access structures mediate who a person can be.

This connects the book to [[Mediated Reality]]. The self must move through informational environments that can classify, expose, or erase it.

## Escaping the System From Inside

The book's technical imagination is not simply anti-technology. Resistance also uses technical knowledge, especially where the system can be turned against its own centralized control.

This distinguishes it from simpler machine dystopias. The issue is not technology as such, but social life redesigned around information control.

That makes the novel a useful bridge from *[[Player Piano]]* to later cyberpunk. Vonnegut worries about automation making people unnecessary; Brunner worries about information systems making people legible, mobile, and governable.

## Why It Belongs Here

*The Shockwave Rider* belongs because it helps bridge older scientific dystopias and later cyberpunk. It shows the secular advance of control moving from machines and laboratories into data, identity, and networks.
