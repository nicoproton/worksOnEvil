# The Movement of World Revolution

***The Movement of World Revolution*** is Christopher Dawson's 1959 study of modern revolutionary movements, especially the spread of revolutionary ideology beyond local political upheaval toward global transformation. It belongs in this graph because it helps connect revolution, secular religion, world consciousness, and total social reconstruction.

## Revolution Becomes World-Historical

Dawson's subject is not only one national revolution. The revolutionary idea becomes a movement with world-historical ambition: history itself is interpreted as a process of emancipation, conflict, and eventual transformation.

This connects the book to [[Progress as Secular Providence]]. The revolutionary future takes on a providential role, judging present suffering and legitimating sacrifice through the promise of a transformed world.

## Revolutionary Culture and Formation

The book belongs near [[World-Consciousness Formation]] because world revolution requires more than seizure of power. It needs education, propaganda, intellectual networks, symbols, myths, and new loyalties.

This does not make Dawson identical to the graph's global-governance branch. His emphasis is revolutionary rather than technocratic. But the common point is formation at a scale larger than the local community or nation.

## Communism as Secular Salvation

Dawson's analysis gives a historical-cultural companion to [[Atheistic Communism]]. Communism is not merely a party program. It can operate as a secular faith with its own account of history, evil, sacrifice, redemption, and final society.

The connection should remain distinct from encyclical condemnation. Dawson gives the movement's cultural and historical form; the encyclicals give the doctrinal and moral judgment.

## Why It Belongs Here

*The Movement of World Revolution* belongs because it clarifies one of the graph's major modern patterns: ideology does not remain theory. It becomes movement, education, allegiance, international network, and cultural imagination. It aims not only to govern existing persons, but to form the consciousness of a world to come.
