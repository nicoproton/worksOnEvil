# America a Prophecy

***America a Prophecy*** is William Blake's 1793 illuminated poem mythologizing the American Revolution as a cosmic eruption of revolutionary energy against empire, repression, and old authority. It belongs in this graph as Blake's prophetic version of political revolution: history is read as spiritual drama rather than merely institutional change.

## Revolution as Cosmic Energy

The American Revolution appears through mythic figures and apocalyptic imagery. Blake does not treat politics as a flat contest of interests. Revolution becomes the release of a bound energy into history.

This connects the poem to [[Progress as Secular Providence]], but Blake's progress is not Godwin's rational reform. It is prophetic upheaval, where the future arrives as fire, awakening, and rupture.

## Orc Against Empire

Orc functions as a figure of revolutionary force against imperial and repressive order. He is not simply a policy program. He is energy, youth, desire, and revolt appearing in mythic form.

This places the work near [[The Overreacher]] only cautiously. Blake wants the reader to feel revolutionary energy as liberation, but the graph can still ask whether released energy can remain ordered toward real good rather than becoming another cycle of domination.

## The Political World as Symbol

The poem's America is not only a nation. It becomes a symbolic field where empire, law, sexuality, religion, and imagination contend.

That gives the poem a relation to [[Hidden Correspondence System]]. Political events are read as outward signs of spiritual and imaginative powers.

## Why It Belongs Here

*America a Prophecy* belongs because it gives the Romantic branch a revolutionary-symbolic node. It shows how political liberation, imaginative energy, and the breaking of old moral forms could be imagined as one event.
