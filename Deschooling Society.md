# Deschooling Society

***Deschooling Society*** is Ivan Illich's 1971 critique of compulsory institutional schooling and the modern identification of education with school attendance, credentials, curriculum, and professionalized instruction. It belongs in this graph because it gives a non-fiction institutional form of a recurring pattern: formation becomes administered, and the institution begins to define the good it was meant to serve.

Illich is not merely arguing for a different school policy. He is asking what happens when learning itself becomes dependent on an institution that classifies persons as educated or uneducated.

## School as Institutionalized Learning

The book's main target is the assumption that education means schooling. Illich argues that school trains people to confuse learning with attendance, knowledge with credentials, and growth with institutional advancement.

This makes the book a strong case of [[Institutional Counterproductivity]]. School exists to serve learning, but can become counterproductive when it teaches dependency on the system that certifies learning.

## Formation by the System

The book also belongs near [[Administered Human Formation]]. School does not simply transmit information. It forms expectations about time, authority, success, expertise, certification, and one's place in the social order.

The connection should remain precise. Illich's target is not identical to Plato's guardians, Brave New World's conditioning, or modernist design schools. His claim is that ordinary institutional education can quietly train people to accept a society organized through institutional dependency.

## Learning Webs

Illich proposes learning webs as an alternative: networks of people, skills, objects, peers, and educators that would let learning happen through voluntary association rather than compulsory institutional progression.

This brings the book close to [[Subsidiarity]] and [[Associational Life]]. The good of learning should be supported by social forms that let persons act, teach, seek, share, and cooperate without being absorbed by a central school apparatus.

## Radical Monopoly of Education

The book also gives one of the clearest examples of [[Radical Monopoly]]. Schooling can become the recognized gateway to education in such a way that knowledge gained outside school becomes socially illegible.

The monopoly is not simply one school system defeating another. The deeper monopoly is the social assumption that learning must pass through school to count.

## Why It Belongs Here

*Deschooling Society* strengthens the graph's education and formation branch. It shows that administered formation need not be openly dystopian to matter. A society can be full of humane intentions while still training persons to seek legitimacy through institutional pathways that weaken local competence, free inquiry, and personal responsibility.
