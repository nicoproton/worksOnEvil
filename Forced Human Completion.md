# Forced Human Completion

Forced Human Completion names the motif in which a system tries to perfect humanity by removing the incompleteness of individual persons. The promised cure may be unity, happiness, peace, rationality, or freedom from loneliness, but the method abolishes some essential human limit: separateness, imagination, suffering, embodiment, or moral choice.

This concept is strongest when the system defines ordinary personhood as a wound to be healed. It overlaps with [[False Salvation Through Systems]], but the emphasis is more specific: completion is imposed from above or engineered through metaphysical, medical, technological, or political force.

Use this link for projects that promise to make humanity whole by making actual human beings less free, less separate, or less capable of relation.

[[Thomas Aquinas]] gives a useful contrast between genuine perfection and imposed completion. A human being is perfected by flourishing according to human goods, not by having difficult human capacities removed for the sake of order.

*[[Serial Experiments Lain]]* gives the motif a networked form: Lain is pressured to become more than a person, a kind of connective principle or god-function for the Wired.

Julian Huxley's [[Conscious Evolution]] gives the motif a non-fiction humanist form. It is not necessarily coercive, but it raises the same question: when humanity becomes a project, who defines completion?
