# Simulated Reality

Simulated Reality names a false or manufactured world that supplies experience while concealing the structure that produces it. The simulation may be digital, psychological, social, or mnemonic.

The motif matters when the false reality is not just a lie but an environment: a place where identity, memory, desire, and social order are stabilized by manufactured appearances.

It overlaps with [[Gnosticism and its Critics]] when the visible world is treated as a prison and liberation depends on awakening to hidden truth.

Cyberpunk works split this motif in useful ways: *The Matrix* makes simulation a total prison, *Ghost in the Shell* makes false reality an implanted interior life, *Serial Experiments Lain* dissolves reality into networked perception, and *Snow Crash* makes the virtual world a second layer of power rather than a complete replacement.

*[[Neuromancer]]* is an important ancestor of this cyberpunk split. Cyberspace is not a total false world, but a shared perceptual environment where data becomes space and power becomes architecture.

[[Secondary World]] is an important contrast. Tolkienian fantasy creates a coherent made world that invites free imaginative participation, while simulated reality in dystopian works often conceals or imprisons.

*Between Two Ages* helps broaden the concept from literal simulation to mediated consciousness: electronic communication can make political reality feel increasingly produced through images, networks, and information environments.

For ordinary political life shaped by news, stereotypes, images, and expert reports rather than total artificial worlds, use [[Mediated Reality]].

When the issue is not a false world but the body being reshaped by media, screens, vehicles, wounds, or technical environments, use [[Media-Flesh Fusion]].

*[[eXistenZ]]* is a hybrid case: game-worlds function as simulation, but entry into them is bodily and organic rather than purely visual or digital.

Philip K. Dick gives the motif its paranoid-Gnostic form. *VALIS* imagines reality itself as prison-like overlay, while *Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?* asks whether technologically mediated religion can still carry real ethical force.

*[[The Three Stigmata of Palmer Eldritch]]* gives the corporate-drug form: simulated consolation for colonists opens into a deeper threat of invasive transcendence.

*[[Permutation City]]* gives the metaphysical-computational form, where simulation is not merely deception but a possible world for artificial life and uploaded persons.
