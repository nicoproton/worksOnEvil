# The Open Conspiracy

***The Open Conspiracy*** is H. G. Wells's 1928 programmatic work calling for an openly declared movement toward a scientifically organized world civilization. It is one of the strongest non-fiction nodes for this vault because it inverts the usual conspiracy form: Wells does not imagine a secret cabal, but an explicit network of educated, scientific, internationalist actors working to supersede existing institutions.

## Open Rather Than Hidden

Wells's phrase "open conspiracy" is important because it changes the meaning of conspiracy. The project is conspiratorial in the sense of coordinated transformation, but it is meant to be public, rational, and evangelistic rather than occult or hidden.

This makes the book a useful counterpoint to [[Layered Conspiracy]]. It shows a form of elite or intellectual coordination that does not require secrecy to be structurally radical.

For a historical model of hidden or semi-hidden elite network structure, compare [[The Anglo-American Establishment]]. Wells is more explicit and programmatic; Quigley is more historical and institutional.

## Scientific World Civilization

The Open Conspiracy aims at a scientifically directed world order beyond nationalism, inherited religion, and traditional political forms. It trusts education, planning, and scientific mentality to reorganize civilization.

This links strongly to [[Scientific World State]] and [[False Salvation Through Systems]]. The promise is peace, rationality, and planetary coordination; the danger is that human life becomes a problem to be administered by those who claim the scientific future.

## Education and Recruitment

Wells places enormous weight on education, propaganda, and the recruitment of capable minds. The movement must shape opinion, form cadres, and create a new public allegiance to world civilization.

This connects the book to [[Engineered Consent]] and [[Elite Network Historiography]]. The transformation of society happens through networks, institutions, schooling, and public persuasion rather than through one spectacular coup.

## Against the Old Order

The book treats existing nation-states, class structures, and inherited loyalties as obsolete obstacles. Its revolutionary character lies in its claim that civilization must be remade at the level of consciousness and institutions.

That makes it relevant to [[Global Problematique]]. Wells sees modern crisis as global in scale and therefore requiring a global reorganization of human thought and power.
