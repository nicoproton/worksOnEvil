# UNESCO - Its Purpose and Its Philosophy

***UNESCO: Its Purpose and Its Philosophy*** is Julian Huxley's 1946 statement of the philosophical aims behind UNESCO. It presents education, science, culture, and international cooperation as instruments for forming a more unified human civilization. The note belongs in this vault because Huxley explicitly links global institutions to evolutionary humanism.

## Evolutionary Humanism

Huxley frames humanity as an evolutionary process becoming conscious of itself. The task of institutions like UNESCO is to help direct that process toward greater unity, knowledge, and human development.

This makes the work a major anchor for [[Evolutionary Humanism]]. Unlike older religious teleologies, the direction of humanity is imagined through biology, culture, education, and planned self-development.

## Education as World Formation

UNESCO's purpose is not only technical cooperation. It is the formation of minds capable of world citizenship. Education becomes a means of shaping shared values, peace, and planetary consciousness.

This links the work to [[Engineered Consent]] and [[Global Problematique]]. The global problem is not only institutional; it is psychological and cultural.

## Unity and Diversity

Huxley argues for world unity while also acknowledging cultural diversity. The tension is important: a global educational project must decide which values are universal and which differences can remain.

This places the text near [[False Salvation Through Systems]]. The ideal is peace through shared human development, but the risk is that unity becomes managed consciousness.

## UNESCO as Soft Technocracy

The work offers a softer version of technocratic power. It is not a police state or machine order, but an educational-cultural apparatus for shaping the future of humanity.

That makes it relevant to [[Scientific World State]] without being identical to Wells. Huxley's emphasis is culture and evolution rather than direct political world government.
