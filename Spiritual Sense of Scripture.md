# Spiritual Sense of Scripture

Spiritual Sense of Scripture names the Catholic and patristic pattern in which Scripture is read as inspired history whose meaning can include literal, allegorical, moral, and anagogical dimensions. It is not a license for arbitrary symbolism. It is the claim that divine authorship makes Scripture deeper than a merely human document.

This bridge belongs near [[Revelation Under Critical Reduction]] and [[Theological Modernism]] as their constructive counter-form. It does not reject historical study; it refuses to let historical method flatten revelation into only psychology, politics, myth, or literary development.

Use this link when:

- Scripture is read through multiple senses ordered by Christ, Church, moral conversion, and final destiny;
- historical study is affirmed but subordinated to divine authorship and ecclesial interpretation;
- reductionist criticism is answered by a richer account of meaning rather than by anti-intellectualism;
- tradition, Fathers, liturgy, and doctrine shape how the text is understood.

Henri de Lubac's *[[Medieval Exegesis]]* is the main modern scholarly anchor. It retrieves the fourfold sense of Scripture and the premodern grammar of spiritual reading.

*[[Providentissimus Deus]]* gives the encyclical boundary: scholarship, languages, manuscripts, and history are valuable, but Scripture must not be treated as merely human because inspiration has been excluded in advance.

Yves Congar's [[Living Tradition]] gives the ecclesial setting. The spiritual reading of Scripture belongs to the Church's living transmission, worship, doctrine, and memory, not to private symbolism alone.

*[[Pascendi Dominici Gregis]]* gives the negative modernist danger. Historical criticism becomes destructive when it relocates revelation into religious consciousness, symbol, or evolutionary development.

This concept should not be used for any symbolic reading. It belongs where the spiritual reading of Scripture is disciplined by Catholic doctrine, tradition, and the unity of revelation.
