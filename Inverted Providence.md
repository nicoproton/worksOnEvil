# Inverted Providence

Inverted Providence names fictional worlds or arguments where moral order appears reversed: virtue is punished, vice is rewarded, and reality itself seems to teach that goodness is delusion. The pattern is not simple tragedy. It is a systematic anti-providence.

This bridge belongs near [[Providential Counter-Revolution]] by contrast. De Maistre reads revolutionary catastrophe as judgment inside a hidden moral order. Sade's darkest fiction often imagines the opposite: a world where cruelty prospers because nature, power, or God has no moral goodness to answer to.

Use this link when:

- the plot repeatedly punishes virtue precisely because it is virtue;
- vice prospers as if it had metaphysical backing;
- providence is mocked, inverted, or replaced by nature as destruction;
- the work makes goodness appear naive because the world itself seems allied with evil.

*[[Justine and Juliette]]* is the central case: Justine's virtue brings suffering, while Juliette's vice brings prosperity. The bridge can be read in argument with [[Privatio Boni]], because the Sadean world often treats evil not as lack but as the law beneath reality.

This concept should not be used for every bleak ending. It belongs where the reversal of moral order is the work's governing philosophical pressure.
