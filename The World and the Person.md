# The World and the Person

***The World and the Person*** is Romano Guardini's 1939 work on Christian anthropology, world, personhood, freedom, and man's relation to reality. It belongs in this graph because it helps clarify a question that runs through many nodes: man is not an isolated consciousness facing raw material, nor a mere function inside a total world-system.

## Personhood Is Not Isolation

Guardini's account of personhood resists the closed self. The person is inward, free, and responsible, but not self-created. He exists in relation to world, neighbor, truth, and God.

This makes the work a useful neighbor of [[Personalist Common Good]]. Maritain gives the social-philosophical version of the person and common good; Guardini gives a more existential and world-directed form.

## The World Is Not Raw Material

The book is also useful because it refuses to treat the world as mere stuff before the will. If the world is received as creation and meaningful order, then human action must begin with reverence and attention rather than command.

This places the work near [[Reason Ordered to Truth]]. Reason does not create truth by organizing material. It receives and answers a world already structured by being, meaning, and divine origin.

## Against Use Without Encounter

Guardini's anthropology also supports [[Encounter vs Manipulation]]. A person can be studied, administered, or used only by first being misrecognized. The real person must be encountered as someone with inwardness, freedom, and a destiny that exceeds the system observing him.

This is why the work belongs beside the graph's anti-technocratic branch. The danger is not only machinery. It is the habit of seeing world and person as available for use before they are received as given.

## Why It Belongs Here

*The World and the Person* gives the Guardini branch its anthropology. *[[Letters from Lake Como]]* shows technical form entering landscape; *[[The End of the Modern World]]* diagnoses modern power; this work clarifies why those changes matter for the person. A world reduced to material and a person reduced to function are two sides of the same spiritual mistake.
