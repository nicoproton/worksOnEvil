# Christian Philosophy

Christian Philosophy names the Catholic pattern in which philosophy remains genuinely rational while receiving aid, protection, and enlargement from Christian revelation. It does not mean that faith replaces argument, or that philosophy becomes disguised theology. It means that reason can be healed and directed by truths it could not securely discover or preserve alone.

This bridge belongs under [[Reason Ordered to Truth]]. It is useful when the graph needs to distinguish Christian use of reason from both rationalism and fideism.

Use this link when:

- reason and revelation are distinguished without being separated into hostile worlds;
- Christian doctrine helps philosophy ask, preserve, or clarify questions about God, creation, freedom, soul, being, personhood, or final end;
- philosophy is protected from skepticism, positivism, myth, or technical reduction by a stronger account of truth;
- Catholic thinkers defend reason without accepting autonomous rationalism.

*[[Aeterni Patris]]* is the encyclical anchor. Leo XIII calls for the restoration of Christian philosophy, especially through [[Thomas Aquinas]], because philosophy shapes theology, education, science, politics, and public judgment.

Etienne Gilson's *[[The Spirit of Medieval Philosophy]]* is the historical-philosophical anchor. Gilson argues that Christianity did not merely borrow Greek philosophy; it generated a distinctive philosophical experience by bringing reason into contact with creation, providence, personhood, freedom, and God as Being.

Gilson's *[[Reason and Revelation in the Middle Ages]]* gives the conflict-and-distinction form. Medieval thought had to hold reason and revelation together without collapsing one into the other or dividing truth against itself.

*[[Fides et Ratio]]* gives the later magisterial form: faith and reason are distinct but mutually necessary, and each weakens when isolated from the other.

This concept should not be used for every Catholic book. It belongs where philosophy itself is at stake.
