# En route

***En route*** is Joris-Karl Huysmans's 1895 Durtal novel about retreat, confession, monastic encounter, and return to Catholicism. It follows *[[La-bas (Down There)]]* not by continuing the occult investigation, but by asking what can heal the soul that has passed through decadent disgust and Satanic fascination.

## After the Black Mass

The importance of *En route* is that it changes the direction of the Durtal sequence. *La-bas* discovers that modern unbelief can still hunger for supernatural intensity and find it in inverted worship. *En route* asks whether that hunger can be rightly ordered.

This makes the novel a strong case of [[Beauty Returned to Worship]]. The issue is not whether Durtal should stop caring about beauty, music, symbol, and ritual. The issue is whether those desires can be removed from decadence and returned to prayer.

## Monastic Discipline

Durtal's retreat at a Trappist monastery gives the novel a structure very different from *Against Nature*. Des Esseintes builds an artificial interior world around his nerves. Durtal enters a rule of life that does not originate in his preferences.

That makes the book an answer to [[Aesthetic Self-Enclosure]]. The self is not saved by arranging more exquisite sensations. It must submit to confession, liturgy, silence, repetition, and the humiliating difficulty of prayer.

## Conversion Without Sentimentality

The novel does not make conversion into instant sweetness. Durtal remains divided, exhausted, and aesthetically sensitive. His return is worked through fear, disgust, longing, resistance, and discipline.

This gives the book a useful relation to [[Disordered Love]]. The same desires that fed decadent fascination are not annihilated; they are gradually re-educated toward a good beyond the self.

## Why It Belongs Here

*En route* belongs because it gives the graph a counter-path to decadence. It shows that the answer to occult inversion is not a flat rationalism or mere respectability, but conversion: beauty, ritual, and longing returned to sacramental order.
